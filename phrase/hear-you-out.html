hear you out

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralOpinions & honesty

To listen to everything someone says before you judge or decide.

I don't love your idea, but I'll hear you out before I decide.

Tip: Use it as hear + person + out ("hear her out", "hear them out"); you can also say "hear me out" to ask someone to be patient before they react.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaKofi, I know you hate the idea of moving the team meeting to eight o'clock.
KofiHate is a strong word, but it's very early for me. I'm taking the train in.
OlenaJust hear me out. If we meet at eight, we're done by nine and everyone gets a quiet morning.
KofiOkay, that's actually a fair point. Could we try it for two weeks?
OlenaSure. Then we can vote on whether to keep it.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "hear me out" mean?

2. Fill the gap

My sister thinks I'm making a mistake with this job offer, so I asked her to before she says no.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: I know you've already made up your mind about the trip, but can I explain my plan?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you disagreed with someone but still listened to their whole point of view.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year my friend wanted to share a flat with two other people, and I thought it was a bad idea. I almost said no straight away, but I decided to hear her out first. In the end her plan made more sense than I expected, and we found a place we all liked.

Answer key: hear you out

  1. 1. C. Listen to my whole explanation before you decide.
  2. 2. hear me out
  3. 3. C. Fine, I'll hear you out, but I can't promise I'll change my mind.
  4. 4. Example: Last year my friend wanted to share a flat with two other people, and I thought it was a bad idea. I almost said no straight away, but I decided to hear her out first. In the end her plan made more sense than I expected, and we found a place we all liked.
