free rein

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

Complete freedom to decide and do things your own way.

My boss gave me free rein to redesign the whole menu.

Tip: Usually used as 'give someone free rein (to do something)' or 'have free rein (over something)'; it suggests real trust, so it's common when talking about managers, clients and parents.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHow's the new job? You sounded nervous last week.
KofiHonestly, it's great. My manager's hardly ever in the office.
OlenaSo who decides how the training program is set up?
KofiI do. She gave me free rein, as long as it's ready by March.
OlenaThat's a lot of trust for someone who just started.
KofiI know. I'm trying not to let it go to my head.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does 'gave me free rein' mean?

2. Fill the gap

The landlord was happy to give us to repaint the flat and choose all the colours ourselves.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: I'm organising my sister's surprise party, and she says I can plan whatever I like.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you were given a lot of freedom to do something your own way. How did you feel about it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I was a student, my professor gave me free rein to choose the topic and method for my final project. It was exciting but a bit scary, because I had nobody telling me what to do. In the end I learned much more than I would have with strict instructions.

Answer key: free rein

  1. 1. A. She let him make his own decisions about how to do the work
  2. 2. free rein
  3. 3. B. Lucky you! It sounds like she's given you free rein.
  4. 4. Example: When I was a student, my professor gave me free rein to choose the topic and method for my final project. It was exciting but a bit scary, because I had nobody telling me what to do. In the end I learned much more than I would have with strict instructions.
