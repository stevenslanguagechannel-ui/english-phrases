second-hand

ExpressionLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

Not new; owned and used by someone else before.

This jacket is second-hand, but it looks brand new.

Tip: Use it as an adjective before a noun ('a second-hand bike') or after 'be' ('it's second-hand'); it can also be an adverb: 'I bought it second-hand.' Don't confuse it with 'second-rate', which means poor quality.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaKofi, you've got a new desk! Did you order it online?
KofiSort of. I found it on a local marketplace app. It's second-hand, but it's solid wood.
OlenaReally? It looks perfect. How much did you pay?
KofiOnly forty pounds. The new ones in the shop were over three hundred.
OlenaWow. Maybe I should look for a bookcase that way too.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does second-hand mean?

2. Fill the gap

I bought my laptop from a colleague who was upgrading, and it still works perfectly.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: Your bike looks great! Was it expensive?

4. Your turn: speak

Do you prefer buying new things or second-hand things? Why?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I usually buy second-hand when it comes to furniture and books, because it's much cheaper and often just as good. Last year I found a second-hand sofa in great condition for a quarter of the shop price. For things like shoes or electronics, though, I'd rather buy new.

Answer key: second-hand

  1. 1. A. Previously owned and used by someone else
  2. 2. second-hand
  3. 3. C. Not at all. I got it second-hand from a neighbour for about fifty euros.
  4. 4. Example: I usually buy second-hand when it comes to furniture and books, because it's much cheaper and often just as good. Last year I found a second-hand sofa in great condition for a quarter of the shop price. For things like shoes or electronics, though, I'd rather buy new.
