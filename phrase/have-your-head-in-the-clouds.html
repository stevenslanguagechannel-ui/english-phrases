have your head in the clouds

IdiomLevel B2CasualWork & study

To be dreaming and not paying attention to real things.

Mei has her head in the clouds. She forgot her keys again.

Tip: Use it with 'have' or 'be' ("He's got his head in the clouds") to describe someone who is dreamy or unrealistic; it's a gentle criticism, so avoid it with your boss.

Hear it in conversation

KenDid you read the notes for tomorrow's presentation?
OlenaNot yet. I keep thinking about my trip to Lisbon next month.
KenOlena, we present at nine. The slides still need work.
OlenaI know, I know. I've had my head in the clouds all week.
KenOkay. Let's sit down together after lunch and fix them.
OlenaThanks, Ken. That would really help.

Answer key: have your head in the clouds

  1. 1. C. To be dreaming about something else and not focusing on what needs doing
  2. 2. head in the clouds
  3. 3. A. Sorry, I had my head in the clouds. I was planning my next holiday.
  4. 4. Example: Last week I went to the shop to buy milk, but I came home with bread and no milk at all. I had my head in the clouds because I was thinking about a trip I'm planning. Now I always write a short list, even for small things.
