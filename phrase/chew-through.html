chew through

Phrasal verbLevel B2CasualTime & effort

To use up or finish a lot of something very quickly.

We chewed through two bags of popcorn before the film even started.

Tip: Use 'chew through + something' for quickly using up or finishing a large amount of something, like food, money, battery, data or paperwork; don't use it for a single small item.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you finish the report for Monday?
KofiNearly. I've got about forty pages of survey results left to check.
OlenaThat's a lot. Will you have time?
KofiI think so. If I skip lunch, I can chew through them by three.
OlenaDon't skip lunch! I'll bring you a sandwich.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about something you or your family use up very quickly at home.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

In my house, we chew through milk really fast. There are four of us, and we all have cereal and tea, so a big bottle is usually gone in two days. I'm always popping out to the shop to buy more.

Answer key: chew through

  2. 2. chew through
  3. 3. B. Streaming videos on the train every day chewed through it.
