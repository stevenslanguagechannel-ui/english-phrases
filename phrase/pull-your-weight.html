pull your weight

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To do your fair share of the work.

Everyone on the team has to pull their weight.

Tip: Use it with a possessive that matches the subject ('I pull my weight', 'she doesn't pull her weight'); it's most often used in negatives or complaints about people who let others do the work.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHow's the group project going? You've got that presentation on Friday, right?
KofiHonestly, I'm stressed. Amira and I have done almost all the research.
OlenaWhat about Diego? Isn't he in your group too?
KofiHe is, but he hasn't pulled his weight at all. He keeps saying he'll send his slides tomorrow.
OlenaThat's not fair on you two. Maybe talk to him before Friday.
KofiYeah, I think we have to. We can't carry him through the whole project.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "hasn't pulled his weight" mean?

2. Fill the gap

We share a flat, and it's only fair that everyone with the cleaning and the cooking.

3. Choose the best reply

Mei: I'm so tired of covering for Sam. He leaves everything to the rest of us.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you worked in a group or shared a space with other people. How did you make sure the work was shared fairly?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I shared a flat with three other students, we made a simple rota so everyone knew their jobs. It worked well because we all pulled our weight, and if someone forgot, we just reminded them politely. I think being clear from the start stops arguments later.

Answer key: pull your weight

  1. 1. B. He hasn't done his fair share of the work.
  2. 2. pulls their weight
  3. 3. A. I know. If he doesn't start pulling his weight, we should talk to the manager.
  4. 4. Example: When I shared a flat with three other students, we made a simple rota so everyone knew their jobs. It worked well because we all pulled our weight, and if someone forgot, we just reminded them politely. I think being clear from the start stops arguments later.
