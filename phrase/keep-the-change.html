keep the change

ExpressionLevel B1CasualEveryday life

You tell someone they can keep the extra money you gave them.

The total is eight dollars. Here's ten. Keep the change.

Tip: Say it right after you hand over more money than the bill; it's a polite way to tell a driver, waiter or cashier not to give you coins back, and it's often a small tip.

Hear it in conversation

KofiThat's twelve fifty for the ride, please.
AmiraHere's a twenty. Keep the change.
KofiReally? Thanks, that's very kind of you.
AmiraNo problem. You got me to the station with ten minutes to spare.
KofiGlad I could help. Have a safe trip!

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you left a little extra money for someone who helped you. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I took a taxi to the airport in heavy rain, and the driver helped me carry my bags. The fare was thirty-two euros, so I gave him forty and said, "Keep the change." He smiled and wished me a good flight.

Answer key: keep the change

  1. 1. B. Amira doesn't need the extra money back, so Kofi can have it.
  2. 2. Keep the change
  3. 3. A. Keep the change. Thanks for waiting for me.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I took a taxi to the airport in heavy rain, and the driver helped me carry my bags. The fare was thirty-two euros, so I gave him forty and said, "Keep the change." He smiled and wished me a good flight.
