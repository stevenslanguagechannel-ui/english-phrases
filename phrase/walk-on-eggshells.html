walk on eggshells

IdiomLevel B2CasualPeople & relationships

To be very careful with what you say or do so you don't upset someone.

Ever since the argument, I've been walking on eggshells around my brother.

Tip: Use it with 'around' or 'on' plus a person or situation (walk on eggshells around someone); it works in any tense, e.g. 'I felt like I was walking on eggshells'.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraHow's the new flat share going?
KofiHonestly, it's tense. My flatmate gets annoyed over the smallest thing.
AmiraLike what?
KofiYesterday I left one mug in the sink and he didn't speak to me all evening. I feel like I'm walking on eggshells every time I'm in the kitchen.
AmiraThat sounds exhausting. Have you tried talking to him about it?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to be very careful about what you said to someone. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few years ago I worked with a colleague who took any criticism very personally. For months I felt like I was walking on eggshells whenever I gave feedback, so I started choosing my words very carefully. In the end we had an honest chat and things got much easier.

Answer key: walk on eggshells

  1. 1. A. Being very careful about what he does so he doesn't upset his flatmate
  2. 2. walking on eggshells
  3. 3. C. I know that feeling. I've been walking on eggshells around my parents when money comes up.
  4. 4. Example: A few years ago I worked with a colleague who took any criticism very personally. For months I felt like I was walking on eggshells whenever I gave feedback, so I started choosing my words very carefully. In the end we had an honest chat and things got much easier.
