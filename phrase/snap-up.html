snap up

Phrasal verbLevel B2CasualEveryday life

To buy or get something quickly because it is a good deal.

If you see a good deal on those shoes, snap it up quickly.

Tip: Use it with a thing as the object: 'snap up a bargain' or 'snap it up'. The thing can go before or after 'up' (snap up tickets / snap them up). It is often used in the passive too: 'The best seats were snapped up.'

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you manage to get tickets for the autumn concert in the park?
KofiJust about. They went on sale at nine and I got two before ten.
OlenaReally? I thought they'd sell out in minutes.
KofiThey nearly did. Most of the cheap ones were snapped up in the first half hour.
OlenaWell, I'm glad you got in there early. Can I pay you back for mine?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you found a really good deal on something. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last spring I saw a good laptop in an online sale at nearly half the usual price. I knew it wouldn't last, so I snapped it up straight away. An hour later it was sold out, so I was really glad I didn't wait.

Answer key: snap up

  1. 1. C. Bought quickly because people wanted them
  2. 2. snapped up
  3. 3. B. Yes, I'm going on Saturday morning before all the good jackets get snapped up.
  4. 4. Example: Last spring I saw a good laptop in an online sale at nearly half the usual price. I knew it wouldn't last, so I snapped it up straight away. An hour later it was sold out, so I was really glad I didn't wait.
