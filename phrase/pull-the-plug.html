pull the plug

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To stop something, often suddenly, so it does not continue.

We had to pull the plug on the online event because of technical problems.

Tip: Use it as 'pull the plug on + project/plan/event', usually when someone with authority decides something isn't worth continuing; it's about stopping, not about electricity, so avoid it for small everyday actions like ending a call.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid the company ever go ahead with the new booking app?
KofiNo, they pulled the plug last month. It was already way over budget.
OlenaReally? I thought the launch was almost ready.
KofiIt was, but the testers kept finding new problems, so management gave up.
OlenaThat's a shame. Your team worked on it for almost a year.
KofiI know, but I'm moving on. We're starting a smaller project next week.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you or someone you know had to stop a plan or project that wasn't working.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few years ago, my friends and I tried to start a small online shop selling handmade gifts. After six months we were barely selling anything and we were all exhausted, so we decided to pull the plug. It was disappointing, but it gave us time to focus on our jobs again.

Answer key: pull the plug

  1. 1. C. Decided to stop the project so it did not continue
  2. 2. pull the plug
  3. 3. C. Honestly, I'd pull the plug on the whole plan and look at other flats.
  4. 4. Example: A few years ago, my friends and I tried to start a small online shop selling handmade gifts. After six months we were barely selling anything and we were all exhausted, so we decided to pull the plug. It was disappointing, but it gave us time to focus on our jobs again.
