leeway

ExpressionLevel C1NeutralWork & study

Extra freedom or flexibility that lets you do something a little differently or later.

My manager gave me some leeway with the deadline.

Tip: Usually used as 'give someone leeway' or 'have some leeway'; it's an uncountable noun, so don't say 'a leeway' or 'leeways'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre you sure you can finish the report by Friday? It's a lot.
KofiI'm trying. The client sent their numbers really late.
OlenaCould you ask for a few extra days?
KofiI already did. They said there's some leeway, as long as I send a draft on Friday.
OlenaThat's a relief. So the draft is enough for now?
KofiYes, and I can polish the rest on Monday.

Show an example answer

When I was moving house last year, my landlord gave me a bit of leeway with the date I had to leave. That meant I didn't have to rush, and I could pack calmly over an extra weekend. I was really grateful, because it took a lot of stress off me.
