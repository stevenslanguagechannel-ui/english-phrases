get to the meat of

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To reach the most important part of something without extra detail.

Let's skip the small talk and get to the meat of the meeting.

Tip: Use it with 'the': 'get to the meat of' is followed by a noun like 'the problem', 'the report' or 'the issue', and people often say 'let's get to the meat of it' to move a conversation along.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaSorry I'm late. Did I miss much of the budget call?
KofiOnly the introductions and some chat about the new software.
OlenaSo nothing important yet?
KofiRight. They're about to get to the meat of it now: who loses money and who gets more.
OlenaOkay, good. Let me turn my camera on and join in.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you were in a long meeting or conversation. How did you feel, and what happened in the end?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I sat through a two-hour planning meeting where the first hour was just updates nobody needed. I was getting restless, so I asked if we could get to the meat of the schedule. After that, we agreed on the deadlines in about twenty minutes.

Answer key: get to the meat of

  1. 1. C. To reach the most important part of the discussion
  2. 2. get to the meat of
  3. 3. B. Understood. I'll get to the meat of it: the flight was cancelled and we need a new plan.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I sat through a two-hour planning meeting where the first hour was just updates nobody needed. I was getting restless, so I asked if we could get to the meat of the schedule. After that, we agreed on the deadlines in about twenty minutes.
