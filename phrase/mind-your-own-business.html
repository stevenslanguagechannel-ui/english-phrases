mind your own business

ExpressionLevel B1CasualPeople & relationships

Stop asking about things that are private and not about you.

I laughed and told him to mind his own business.

Tip: Use 'mind your own business' (or 'mind my/his/her own business') to say that someone is asking about private things; it can sound rude when said directly, so friends usually say it with a joke or a smile.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaKofi, did you see Amira's new car outside? It must have cost a fortune.
KofiProbably. Why don't you ask her how much she paid?
OlenaI did, but she just smiled and said I should mind my own business.
KofiHa! Fair enough. Money is private.
OlenaI know, I know. I was only curious.
KofiCurious is fine. Just wait until she tells you herself.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when someone asked you a question that felt too personal. What did you do?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A colleague once asked me exactly how much I paid for my flat. I didn't want to be rude, so I smiled and said it was private. If she had kept asking, I would have told her to mind her own business.

Answer key: mind your own business

  1. 1. D. Stop asking about private things that don't involve you
  2. 2. mind her own business
  3. 3. B. That's none of your concern, so please mind your own business.
  4. 4. Example: A colleague once asked me exactly how much I paid for my flat. I didn't want to be rude, so I smiled and said it was private. If she had kept asking, I would have told her to mind her own business.
