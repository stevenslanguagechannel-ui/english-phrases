shake off

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralEveryday life

To get rid of an illness or a bad feeling.

I can't shake off this cold.

Tip: Use 'shake off' with an object: 'shake off a cold/a bad mood/a feeling', and with a pronoun it splits: 'I can't shake it off'.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraYou still look tired, Ken. Weren't you ill last week?
KenYeah, it's just a cough now, but I can't seem to shake it off.
AmiraHave you tried resting properly for a couple of days?
KenI'd love to, but we've got the client visit on Thursday.
AmiraThen at least go home on time tonight and get some sleep.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you felt down or unwell and it took a while to get better. What helped?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last winter I caught a heavy cold, and for nearly two weeks I couldn't shake it off. What finally helped was taking a few quiet days at home, drinking lots of tea and sleeping early.

Answer key: shake off

  1. 1. C. To get rid of something unpleasant that keeps lingering
  2. 2. shake off
  3. 3. B. Try to shake it off. One bad game doesn't mean much.
  4. 4. Example: Last winter I caught a heavy cold, and for nearly two weeks I couldn't shake it off. What finally helped was taking a few quiet days at home, drinking lots of tea and sleeping early.
