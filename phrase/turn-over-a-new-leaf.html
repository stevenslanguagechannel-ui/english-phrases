turn over a new leaf

IdiomLevel B2NeutralSuccess & progress

To change your behavior and start doing better.

After the holidays, I'm going to turn over a new leaf and exercise more.

Tip: Use 'turn over a new leaf' (often 'turn over a new leaf and + verb') when someone decides to change their habits or behavior for the better; don't use it for simply starting a new activity or moving to a new place.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look different, Ken. You're not rushing in at nine fifteen anymore!
KenI know. After that awkward meeting with my manager, I decided to turn over a new leaf.
OlenaSeriously? What changed?
KenI set two alarms, I pack my bag the night before, and I leave twenty minutes earlier.
OlenaWell, it's working. You even had time to make coffee for everyone.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you decided to change a habit. What did you do, and how did it go?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A couple of years ago I realised I was staying up far too late on my phone, so I decided to turn over a new leaf. I started leaving my phone in the kitchen at night and going to bed at eleven. It was hard for the first week, but now I wake up feeling much more energetic.

Answer key: turn over a new leaf

  1. 1. C. Start behaving in a better, more responsible way than before
  2. 2. turned over a new leaf
  3. 3. A. Then maybe it's time to turn over a new leaf and start cooking at home.
  4. 4. Example: A couple of years ago I realised I was staying up far too late on my phone, so I decided to turn over a new leaf. I started leaving my phone in the kitchen at night and going to bed at eleven. It was hard for the first week, but now I wake up feeling much more energetic.
