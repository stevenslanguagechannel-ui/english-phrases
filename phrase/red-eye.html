red-eye

ExpressionLevel B2CasualEveryday life

A red-eye is a flight that leaves late at night and lands early in the morning.

I took the red-eye to London, so I was tired when I landed.

Tip: Use 'the red-eye' or 'a red-eye' as a noun, usually with 'take' or 'catch'; it only describes a flight, not a train or bus, and it's mostly American English, though people understand it everywhere.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraAre you sure you want to fly out tonight? You've got the client meeting at nine tomorrow.
KofiIt's the cheapest option. I'll catch the red-eye and go straight to the office from the airport.
AmiraYou won't sleep at all, though.
KofiI'll nap on the plane. If I'm a zombie in the meeting, just pass me a coffee.
AmiraFine, but don't present the budget slides half asleep.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you travelled somewhere and had to deal with a very early arrival or a lack of sleep.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I took the red-eye to visit my cousin, and I barely slept on the plane. We landed at five in the morning, so I spent the first day drinking coffee and trying to stay awake. Next time I'll pay a bit more for a daytime flight.

Answer key: red-eye

  1. 1. B. A flight that leaves late at night and arrives early in the morning
  2. 2. red-eye
  3. 3. A. I took the red-eye on Sunday night and landed around six.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I took the red-eye to visit my cousin, and I barely slept on the plane. We landed at five in the morning, so I spent the first day drinking coffee and trying to stay awake. Next time I'll pay a bit more for a daytime flight.
