bite off more than you can chew

IdiomLevel B2CasualTime & effort

To try to do more than you can manage.

I took on three projects and bit off more than I could chew.

Tip: Use 'bite off more than you can chew' (often in the past: 'I think I bit off more than I could chew') when you took on too much work or responsibility; it's about commitments, not food.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look exhausted, Kofi. Are you okay?
KofiHonestly, no. I'm studying for my exam, working extra shifts, and I agreed to help my cousin move this weekend.
OlenaThat's a lot for one week.
KofiI know. I think I bit off more than I could chew this time.
OlenaMaybe ask your cousin if the move can wait until next week?
KofiGood idea. I'll text him tonight.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you took on too much. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few years ago I agreed to organise my friend's surprise party while I was also starting a new job. I really bit off more than I could chew, and I ended up asking two other friends to help with the food and invitations. It was stressful, but I learned to say no more often.

Answer key: bite off more than you can chew

  1. 1. A. He agreed to more things than he can realistically handle.
  2. 2. bitten off more than she can chew
  3. 3. A. Sounds like you've bitten off more than you can chew. Can anyone take one of those jobs?
  4. 4. Example: A few years ago I agreed to organise my friend's surprise party while I was also starting a new job. I really bit off more than I could chew, and I ended up asking two other friends to help with the food and invitations. It was stressful, but I learned to say no more often.
