pick up on

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralPeople & relationships

To notice something small or hidden, like a feeling or a change.

She picked up on his nervous tone right away.

Tip: Use 'pick up on + noun' (pick up on the tension, a change, a hint); it often describes noticing something that nobody said out loud.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you talk to Kofi after the meeting? He seemed really quiet.
DiegoYeah. I think he's worried about his project, but he didn't say it.
OlenaHow do you know, then?
DiegoI picked up on the way he kept checking his phone and changing the subject.
OlenaGood point. Maybe I'll invite him for lunch tomorrow.
DiegoDo that. I think he'd really appreciate it.

4. Your turn: speak

Can you describe a time when you noticed that someone was upset, even though they didn't say so?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month, my colleague seemed less cheerful than usual during our team call. I picked up on the fact that she wasn't joking around and her answers were very short. Afterwards I sent her a message, and she told me she'd had a really stressful week.

Answer key: pick up on

  1. 1. D. Noticed small signs that showed how someone felt
  2. 2. pick up on
  3. 3. C. Well, she picked up on it because I kept tapping my foot under the table.
  4. 4. Example: Last month, my colleague seemed less cheerful than usual during our team call. I picked up on the fact that she wasn't joking around and her answers were very short. Afterwards I sent her a message, and she told me she'd had a really stressful week.
