a rabbit hole

IdiomLevel B2CasualTime & effort

A topic or activity that pulls you in so deeply that you lose lots of time.

I opened one cooking video and fell down a rabbit hole for two hours.

Tip: Usually used as 'fall down a rabbit hole' or 'go down a rabbit hole', and it's often about browsing or researching online; it can sound a bit negative or playfully self-critical.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you finish the travel plan for Lisbon?
KofiNot even close. I only wanted to check one train time.
AmiraAnd then?
KofiThen I ended up in a rabbit hole of tiny hotels and walking tours, and suddenly it was midnight.
AmiraClassic. Send me the hotel you liked best and I'll take a look tomorrow.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does a rabbit hole mean?

2. Fill the gap

I only meant to read one article about sleep, but I fell down a and watched videos until two in the morning.

3. Choose the best reply

Ken: I only wanted to check the weather, and now I've been reading about volcanoes for an hour.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you started something small and it took much more time than you expected.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I wanted to fix one setting on my phone, and I fell down a rabbit hole of forums and videos. Three hours later I had learned a lot, but I still hadn't fixed the setting. It's funny now, but I was annoyed that I'd lost my whole evening.

Answer key: a rabbit hole

  1. 1. C. A topic that keeps pulling you in, so you spend far more time on it than planned
  2. 2. rabbit hole
  3. 3. C. Ha, that's a real rabbit hole. Did you at least learn something?
  4. 4. Example: Last month I wanted to fix one setting on my phone, and I fell down a rabbit hole of forums and videos. Three hours later I had learned a lot, but I still hadn't fixed the setting. It's funny now, but I was annoyed that I'd lost my whole evening.
