ring a bell

IdiomLevel B2CasualEveryday life

To sound familiar, so you feel you have heard it before.

Her name rings a bell, but I can't remember where I met her.

Tip: Use it as 'X rings a bell' or 'Does X ring a bell?' for names, places, faces or stories; it means you feel you know it, but you usually can't say exactly where from.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHave you met the new designer yet? Her name is Priya Nair.
KofiPriya Nair... that rings a bell. Did she work at the agency across the street?
OlenaMaybe. She said she's lived in three cities this year.
KofiNow I remember! She gave a talk at the conference in March.
OlenaGreat, then you already have something to chat about at lunch.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: Do you remember a restaurant called Little Olive? We went there once, I think.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you met someone, or heard a name, that seemed familiar but you couldn't remember why.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month a colleague introduced me to a man called Daniel, and his name really rang a bell. I spent the whole meeting trying to remember where I knew him from. In the end I realised we had studied in the same building years ago.

Answer key: ring a bell

  1. 1. B. The name sounds familiar to Kofi, but he isn't sure why.
  2. 2. familiar
  3. 3. C. Hmm, Little Olive... that rings a bell, but I can't picture the place.
  4. 4. Example: Last month a colleague introduced me to a man called Daniel, and his name really rang a bell. I spent the whole meeting trying to remember where I knew him from. In the end I realised we had studied in the same building years ago.
