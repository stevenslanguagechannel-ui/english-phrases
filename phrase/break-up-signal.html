break up (signal)

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

When a voice breaks up on a call, the sound keeps cutting out because the signal is weak.

You're breaking up. Can you say that again?

Tip: Use it with the person or the line as the subject: 'You're breaking up' or 'The call keeps breaking up'; it describes sound or video cutting out, not the call ending.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHi Kofi, can you hear me? I'm on the train.
KofiBarely. Your voice keeps breaking up every few seconds.
OlenaSorry, we're going through a tunnel. Is it better now?
KofiNot really. I only caught 'meeting' and 'ten o'clock'.
OlenaI'll text you the details when I get a signal.
KofiPerfect, thanks.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when technology didn't work well while you were talking to someone online.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I had an online interview, and my internet was really unstable. The interviewer's voice kept breaking up, so I had to ask her to repeat several questions. In the end I switched to my phone's data and it worked fine.

Answer key: break up (signal)

  1. 1. B. Olena's voice keeps cutting out because the signal is weak
  2. 2. breaking up
  3. 3. C. Sorry, the line's really bad. I think I'm breaking up. Let me move closer to the window.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I had an online interview, and my internet was really unstable. The interviewer's voice kept breaking up, so I had to ask her to repeat several questions. In the end I switched to my phone's data and it worked fine.
