have a blast

ExpressionLevel B1CasualFeelings & reactions

To enjoy yourself a lot.

We had a blast at Kofi's birthday party.

Tip: Use it as 'have a blast' (or 'had a blast') plus an optional 'at/on/doing': 'We had a blast at the beach'; it's about enjoying an event or activity, not a thing or a person.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraHow was the school trip to the science museum, Ken?
KenHonestly, I was nervous about taking thirty kids on a train.
AmiraAnd? Did it go okay?
KenIt was great! They had a blast building tiny rockets, and nobody got lost.
AmiraThat's a relief. I'd have been so stressed!

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does 'had a blast' mean?

2. Fill the gap

I thought the hiking weekend would be boring, but we actually and I want to go again next month.

3. Choose the best reply

Olena: How was your first week at the new gym with your friends?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you did something with friends or family that you really enjoyed.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last summer my cousins and I spent a weekend at a lake house. We swam, cooked together and played board games until late, and honestly we had a blast. I'd love to do it again next year.

Answer key: have a blast

  1. 1. A. Enjoyed themselves a lot
  2. 2. had a blast
  3. 3. A. We had a blast! The group class was so much fun.
  4. 4. Example: Last summer my cousins and I spent a weekend at a lake house. We swam, cooked together and played board games until late, and honestly we had a blast. I'd love to do it again next year.
