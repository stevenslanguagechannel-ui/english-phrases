fall behind

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralWork & study

To make less progress than the others in a group.

I was sick for a week, so I fell behind in class.

Tip: Use 'fall behind' alone or with 'on' or 'with' (fall behind on rent, fall behind with the project); the past form is 'fell behind', and the opposite is 'keep up'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHey Kofi, are you coming to the gym tonight?
KofiI can't. We moved offices this month and I've fallen behind on my reports.
OlenaOh no. Is your manager worried?
KofiA bit. I just need two quiet evenings to catch up.
OlenaThen go home and get started. We can go on Saturday instead.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had too much to do and couldn't keep up. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last spring I started a new job and a evening course at the same time. After a few weeks I began to fall behind with my homework, because I was always tired after work. In the end I made a weekly plan, and that helped me catch up.

Answer key: fall behind

  1. 1. C. I have done less of my reports than I should have by now.
  2. 2. fall behind
  3. 3. B. Same here. I've fallen behind on my fitness goals this year.
  4. 4. Example: Last spring I started a new job and a evening course at the same time. After a few weeks I began to fall behind with my homework, because I was always tired after work. In the end I made a weekly plan, and that helped me catch up.
