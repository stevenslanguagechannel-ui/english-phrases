take the fall

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To accept the blame for a mistake, especially one other people share.

The team made the mistake together, but Ken took the fall for it.

Tip: Use 'take the fall (for something)' when someone accepts blame, often unfairly or on behalf of others; it often suggests the person was protecting the group or was pushed into it.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you see the email from the client? They're furious about the missing invoices.
DiegoYes. Honestly, three of us skipped the final check, not just Amira.
OlenaI know, but she told the manager it was all her fault.
DiegoThat's not fair. She shouldn't have to take the fall for all of us.
OlenaAgreed. Let's go and talk to the manager together.

1. What does it mean?

2. Fill the gap

Nobody wanted to admit the error, so in the end Priya had to for the whole group.

3. Choose the best reply

Kofi: The report went out with the wrong figures, and my manager is asking who is responsible.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when a group made a mistake. Who was blamed, and how did you feel about it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year, our group project was handed in late because we all misjudged the deadline. Our team leader took the fall and apologised to the professor, even though it wasn't only his fault. I thought that was generous, so I made sure to thank him and support him afterwards.

Answer key: take the fall

  1. 1. D. Accept the blame for a mistake, even when others share it
  2. 2. take the fall
  3. 3. A. Don't worry, I'll take the fall. It was a team mistake anyway.
  4. 4. Example: Last year, our group project was handed in late because we all misjudged the deadline. Our team leader took the fall and apologised to the professor, even though it wasn't only his fault. I thought that was generous, so I made sure to thank him and support him afterwards.
