a hard pill to swallow

IdiomLevel B2NeutralFeelings & reactions

A fact or piece of news that is difficult or upsetting to accept.

Losing the final by one point was a hard pill to swallow.

Tip: Say 'X was a hard pill to swallow' about news or a result you had to accept, not about a person; it often follows a disappointment, and you can add 'but' to show you have moved on.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you hear back from the housing office about your flat?
KofiYes. They're knocking the building down next spring, so everyone has to move out.
AmiraOh no. After six years there?
KofiI know. It's a hard pill to swallow, honestly. I really loved that place.
AmiraI'm sorry. Do you want help looking at new apartments this weekend?
KofiThat would be great. Thanks.

Show an example answer

A few years ago I found out that my favourite teacher was leaving our school halfway through the year. It was a hard pill to swallow because I'd really been enjoying her classes. In the end I wrote her a thank-you card and tried to focus on the rest of the term.

Answer key: a hard pill to swallow

  1. 1. A. News that is difficult and upsetting to accept
  2. 2. a hard pill to swallow
  3. 3. C. That's a hard pill to swallow, especially after such a long trip.
  4. 4. Example: A few years ago I found out that my favourite teacher was leaving our school halfway through the year. It was a hard pill to swallow because I'd really been enjoying her classes. In the end I wrote her a thank-you card and tried to focus on the rest of the term.
