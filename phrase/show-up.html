show up

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

To arrive, or to appear, especially when people are waiting for you.

Ken showed up ten minutes late to the meeting.

Tip: Use 'show up' (without an object) for arriving or appearing, often with a place or time: 'show up at the party', 'show up late'; don't say 'show up the party' or use it to mean 'show' something to someone.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid the plumber ever come this morning?
DiegoNot yet. He said between nine and twelve, and it's almost one.
AmiraHave you called the office?
DiegoTwice. They told me he'd show up soon, but I'm still waiting.
AmiraGive it another half hour, then ask them to send someone else.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time someone you were waiting for didn't arrive on time. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I was meeting a friend for lunch, and she didn't show up for almost an hour. I tried calling her, but her phone was off. In the end she arrived with a long story about a cancelled train, and we both laughed about it.

Answer key: show up

  1. 1. B. Arrive at the place where people expect him
  2. 2. showed up
  3. 3. C. She said she'd show up around quarter past, so let's order some water.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I was meeting a friend for lunch, and she didn't show up for almost an hour. I tried calling her, but her phone was off. In the end she arrived with a long story about a cancelled train, and we both laughed about it.
