fly by the seat of your pants

IdiomLevel B2CasualWork & study

To fly by the seat of your pants is to do something without a plan and decide as you go.

We had no plan in Rome, so we just flew by the seat of our pants.

Tip: Use it for how someone does something (often 'fly by the seat of your pants' + a task or time), and keep the verb in the right tense: 'flew by the seat of my pants'. It's about improvising, not about being reckless.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHow did the workshop go on Friday? I thought the printer broke.
KofiIt did, and my slides were stuck on it. I had nothing prepared.
OlenaOh no. So what did you do?
KofiI flew by the seat of my pants, honestly. I just asked everyone about their problems and went from there.
OlenaAnd people liked it?
KofiThey did! Two of them even asked for another session.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had to do something without much preparation. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year my manager asked me to lead a client call because a colleague was ill. I had about ten minutes to get ready, so I basically flew by the seat of my pants and answered questions as they came up. It went better than I expected, and the client even thanked me afterwards.

Answer key: fly by the seat of your pants

  1. 1. D. I improvised and made decisions as I went, without a plan
  2. 2. fly by the seat of my pants
  3. 3. C. Not really, we flew by the seat of our pants and picked places each morning.
  4. 4. Example: Last year my manager asked me to lead a client call because a colleague was ill. I had about ten minutes to get ready, so I basically flew by the seat of my pants and answered questions as they came up. It went better than I expected, and the client even thanked me afterwards.
