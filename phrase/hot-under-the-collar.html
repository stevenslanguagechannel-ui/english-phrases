hot under the collar

IdiomLevel B2CasualFeelings & reactions

Angry or annoyed, often over something small.

My brother gets hot under the collar whenever someone parks in his spot.

Tip: Use it with get or be ("he gets hot under the collar"), usually about a person's reaction to something minor; it sounds odd for serious anger.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you see Ken's reply to the group email about the meeting room?
DiegoI did. All we asked was whether he could swap rooms on Thursday.
AmiraI know. He got really hot under the collar about it.
DiegoMaybe he's just stressed. He has that deadline on Friday.
AmiraTrue. I'll bring him a coffee and say sorry for the short notice.
DiegoGood idea. That should calm things down.

Show an example answer

My cousin got really hot under the collar last month when a delivery arrived an hour late. It was only a parcel of books, but he had planned his whole afternoon around it. Looking back, he laughed about it, but at the time he was quite upset.

Answer key: hot under the collar

  1. 1. B. Annoyed or angry, even though the reason seems small
  2. 2. hot under the collar
  3. 3. C. He got hot under the collar when they wouldn't accept his coupon.
  4. 4. Example: My cousin got really hot under the collar last month when a delivery arrived an hour late. It was only a parcel of books, but he had planned his whole afternoon around it. Looking back, he laughed about it, but at the time he was quite upset.
