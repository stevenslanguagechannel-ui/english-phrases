wear many hats

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To have many different jobs or roles.

In a small café, you wear many hats: cook, cashier and cleaner.

Tip: Use 'wear many hats' (or 'wear a lot of hats') about jobs, volunteering or family roles; it usually describes a situation, so it often follows 'have to' or 'end up': 'I end up wearing many hats.'

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look exhausted, Kofi. How's the new job going?
KofiBusy! The company only has eight people, so I wear many hats.
OlenaLike what?
KofiThis morning I fixed the printer, answered customer emails and booked a client lunch.
OlenaThat's a lot for one person. Are you at least learning plenty?
KofiDefinitely. I just wish I had a bit more time for my main project.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to do several different jobs at once, at work, at home or in a club.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I helped organise my cousin's wedding, I had to wear many hats. I was the planner, the driver and even the person who calmed everyone down. It was tiring, but I learned a lot about managing different tasks.

Answer key: wear many hats

  1. 1. A. To do several different jobs or roles at the same time
  2. 2. wear many hats
  3. 3. B. Wow, you really wear many hats these days!
  4. 4. Example: When I helped organise my cousin's wedding, I had to wear many hats. I was the planner, the driver and even the person who calmed everyone down. It was tiring, but I learned a lot about managing different tasks.
