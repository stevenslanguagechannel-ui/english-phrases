make a racket

ExpressionLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To make a loud, annoying noise.

Stop making such a racket. I'm trying to sleep.

Tip: Use it as 'make a racket' or 'make such a racket' about people, animals or machines; it's informal, so avoid it in very formal writing.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you sleep okay last night?
KofiNot really. The people upstairs were moving furniture until midnight.
OlenaAgain? They always seem to make a racket on weeknights.
KofiI know. I might knock on their door tonight.
OlenaBe polite, though. Maybe they don't realise how thin the floors are.
KofiGood point. I'll just ask them to move things a bit more quietly.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "make a racket" mean?

2. Fill the gap

The builders next door always before eight in the morning, so I never need an alarm clock.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: Why are you wearing headphones at home?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a place where noise sometimes bothers you. What happens, and how do you deal with it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

In my building, the neighbours' dog tends to make a racket whenever someone walks past the front door. It's hard to concentrate when I'm studying at home. Usually I put on headphones, or I go to the library where it's much quieter.

Answer key: make a racket

  1. 1. C. Make a lot of loud, annoying noise
  2. 2. make a racket
  3. 3. A. Because the kids are making such a racket in the next room.
  4. 4. Example: In my building, the neighbours' dog tends to make a racket whenever someone walks past the front door. It's hard to concentrate when I'm studying at home. Usually I put on headphones, or I go to the library where it's much quieter.
