elbow grease

IdiomLevel B2CasualEveryday life

Hard physical work, especially when you clean something.

This floor needs a lot of elbow grease.

Tip: Use it as an uncountable noun, usually with 'need', 'take' or 'put some ... into': 'It just takes a bit of elbow grease.' It's friendly and a little old-fashioned, so it suits chatty speech more than formal writing.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you get the burn marks off that old baking tray?
KofiEventually. I tried three different cleaners, but they didn't do much.
AmiraSo what finally worked?
KofiJust baking soda and a lot of elbow grease. My arms still ache.
AmiraWow, it looks almost brand new!

Tell me about a time you had to clean or fix something at home that took a lot of effort.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last spring I tried to clean the old bathroom tiles in my flat, and they were covered in stubborn marks. Strong cleaners didn't help much, so in the end it just took a lot of elbow grease and an old toothbrush. It took me a whole afternoon, but the result was worth it.
