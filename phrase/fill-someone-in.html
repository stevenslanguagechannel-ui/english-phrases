fill someone in

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualWork & study

To give someone the latest information they missed.

I missed the meeting. Can you fill me in?

Tip: Use the pattern 'fill someone in (on something)'; with a pronoun, it splits: 'fill me in', not 'fill in me'. Don't confuse it with 'fill in a form', which means something different.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraSorry I'm late. Did I miss anything important?
KenA bit. The client changed the deadline.
AmiraWait, what? Since when?
KenSince this morning. I'll fill you in on the details after the call.
AmiraThanks. I'll grab a coffee and meet you in the break room.

Answer key: fill someone in

  1. 1. D. Tell you what you missed
  2. 2. fill me in
  3. 3. C. Yes, quite a lot! Let me fill you in over lunch.
