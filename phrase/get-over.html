get over

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralFeelings & reactions

To become well again after an illness or a bad time.

It took me a week to get over the flu.

Tip: Use 'get over' + a noun (an illness, a breakup, a shock) or 'get over it' when the thing is already known; it is never followed by a person you're comparing to.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraHey Kofi, you look much better. Are you back at full speed?
KofiAlmost. That cold was awful, but I've finally got over it.
AmiraGood. We missed you at the Thursday meeting.
KofiI know. I'll catch up on everything this afternoon.
AmiraNo rush. Just don't push yourself too hard on your first day back.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had a difficult experience. How did you deal with it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few years ago I failed an important exam, and I was really disappointed for weeks. It took me a while to get over it, but I talked to friends and decided to try again. The second time I passed, and I felt much more confident.

Answer key: get over

  1. 1. D. Recovered from it and felt well again
  2. 2. get over
  3. 3. B. Oh, come on, you need to get over it. It was only one train!
  4. 4. Example: A few years ago I failed an important exam, and I was really disappointed for weeks. It took me a while to get over it, but I talked to friends and decided to try again. The second time I passed, and I felt much more confident.
