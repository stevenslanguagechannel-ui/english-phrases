cabin fever

IdiomLevel B2CasualFeelings & reactions

The restless, bored feeling you get from staying inside for too long.

After three snowy days at home, I've got cabin fever.

Tip: Say 'I've got cabin fever' or 'I'm getting cabin fever' for yourself; it's used for being stuck indoors (bad weather, illness, lockdown), not for feeling bored at work or in a busy place.

Hear it in conversation

KofiYou look tired, Olena. Did you sleep badly?
OlenaA bit. I've been working from my flat all week because of the storm.
KofiHave you been out at all?
OlenaOnly to the bin. I think I'm getting cabin fever, honestly. I keep tidying shelves just to move around.
KofiCome to the café with me tomorrow. The rain should stop by the morning.
OlenaYes, please. I need to see other people.

3. Choose the best reply

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to stay indoors for a long time. How did you feel?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few winters ago I was ill and stuck at home for nearly two weeks. By the end I had terrible cabin fever, and I was walking from room to room with nothing to do. The first thing I did when I felt better was go for a long walk, even though it was freezing.

Answer key: cabin fever

  1. 1. C. A restless, bored feeling from being stuck indoors for too long
  2. 2. cabin fever
  3. 3. C. Poor you. You must be getting cabin fever by now!
  4. 4. Example: A few winters ago I was ill and stuck at home for nearly two weeks. By the end I had terrible cabin fever, and I was walking from room to room with nothing to do. The first thing I did when I felt better was go for a long walk, even though it was freezing.
