snowed under

IdiomLevel B2CasualWork & study

To have so much work that you cannot deal with it all.

I can't come tonight. I'm snowed under with work.

Tip: Use it as 'be snowed under (with + thing)', usually about work, emails or tasks; it describes a busy period, not one busy moment, and it sounds natural in both speech and informal writing.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraAre you coming to Olena's housewarming on Saturday?
KofiI'd love to, but I'm completely snowed under at the moment.
AmiraIs it the new client again?
KofiYes. Two reports are due Monday and I haven't started the second one.
AmiraOuch. Come for an hour if you finish early.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had too much to do. How did you cope?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last spring I was completely snowed under because I was finishing a big project at work while also studying for an exam. I made a list every morning and did the most urgent things first. It was a stressful month, but I learned to ask for help instead of trying to do everything myself.

Answer key: snowed under

  1. 1. A. Kofi has more work than he can manage.
  2. 2. snowed under
  3. 3. B. Yes, I'm snowed under at the moment. I've got three deadlines this week.
  4. 4. Example: Last spring I was completely snowed under because I was finishing a big project at work while also studying for an exam. I made a list every morning and did the most urgent things first. It was a stressful month, but I learned to ask for help instead of trying to do everything myself.
