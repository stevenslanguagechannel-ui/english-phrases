cut corners

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To do something in a cheaper or quicker way, usually with lower quality.

Don't cut corners when you paint the wall, or it will look bad.

Tip: Use it as a verb phrase (cut corners, cutting corners, cut a few corners), usually with a negative or a warning; it often suggests the result will suffer, so don't use it as a compliment.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid the contractor finish the bathroom?
KofiYes, but I'm not happy. The tiles are already coming loose.
OlenaAlready? It's only been a month.
KofiI think they cut corners to finish before the weekend.
OlenaThen you should ask them to fix it for free.
KofiI will. I'm calling them tomorrow.

4. Your turn: speak

Is there anything you think people should never do quickly or cheaply? Why?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I think people should never cut corners when it comes to safety, for example when they repair a car or fix something electrical. It might save a bit of time or money, but a small mistake could cause a serious accident. I'd rather pay more and know the job was done properly.

Answer key: cut corners

  1. 1. A. Did the job quickly or cheaply, so the quality was poor
  2. 2. cut corners
  3. 3. A. Sorry, I was in a rush and cut corners on the checking.
  4. 4. Example: I think people should never cut corners when it comes to safety, for example when they repair a car or fix something electrical. It might save a bit of time or money, but a small mistake could cause a serious accident. I'd rather pay more and know the job was done properly.
