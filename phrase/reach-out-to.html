reach out to

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralPeople & relationships

To contact someone, often to offer or ask for help or just to say hello.

If you need help with the form, please reach out to me.

Tip: Use 'reach out to + person' (or just 'reach out' when the person is clear); it sounds friendly and is common at work and with friends, and don't confuse it with the physical 'reach out your hand'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHave you heard from Kofi lately? He moved to Toronto in the spring.
AmiraNot really. I keep meaning to message him, but I never know what to say.
OlenaJust say hi. I'm sure he'd love to hear from you.
AmiraYou're right. I'll reach out to him this weekend and ask how the new job is going.
OlenaGood idea. Tell him we should all have a video call soon.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you contacted someone for help or advice. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I started my new job, I felt lost with the software, so I reached out to a colleague who had used it for years. She sent me a short guide and offered to answer any questions. It made me feel much more confident, and I'm glad I didn't wait.

Answer key: reach out to

  1. 1. D. Contact him to say hello and ask how he is
  2. 2. reach out to
  3. 3. C. Why don't you reach out to your classmates? Someone might be able to help.
  4. 4. Example: When I started my new job, I felt lost with the software, so I reached out to a colleague who had used it for years. She sent me a short guide and offered to answer any questions. It made me feel much more confident, and I'm glad I didn't wait.
