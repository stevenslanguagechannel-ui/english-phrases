stay away from

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

To avoid something or not go near it.

I'm trying to stay away from sweets this month.

Tip: Use it with a noun or an -ing form: 'stay away from the city centre', 'stay away from arguing'; don't forget 'from'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre you coming to the market on Saturday? It's going to be so busy.
KofiI'd love to, but my knee is still sore. The doctor said I should stay away from crowds for a while.
OlenaOh, that makes sense. Someone could easily knock into you.
KofiExactly. I'll go next week, when it's quieter.
OlenaOkay, I'll bring you some fresh bread instead.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does stay away from mean?

2. Fill the gap

My brother has a cold, so I'm going to him until he feels better.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: Do you want to try the new cafe by the station? It's always full of loud students.

4. Your turn: speak

Is there a food, a habit or a place that you try to avoid? Tell me about it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I try to stay away from my phone before bed. When I scroll late at night, I sleep badly the next day. So now I leave it in the kitchen and read a book instead.

Answer key: stay away from

  1. 1. C. Avoid being near something
  2. 2. stay away from
  3. 3. B. Thanks, but I'm trying to stay away from busy places this week.
  4. 4. Example: I try to stay away from my phone before bed. When I scroll late at night, I sleep badly the next day. So now I leave it in the kitchen and read a book instead.
