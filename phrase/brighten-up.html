brighten up

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

When the weather brightens up, the sky gets lighter and the clouds go away.

The weather should brighten up in the afternoon.

Tip: Use it with weather or skies as the subject ("it brightens up"), often with a time like "later" or "by lunchtime"; it describes a change from grey or cloudy to lighter.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraAre we still going to the market this morning? It's so grey out there.
KenThe forecast says it'll brighten up around eleven.
AmiraGood. I don't want to carry shopping bags in the rain.
KenLet's have a slow breakfast first, then head out.
AmiraPerfect. I'll make some more toast.

Describe a time when bad weather spoiled your plans. What happened in the end?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last summer we planned a picnic in the park, but it was grey and drizzly all morning. We waited in a café for an hour, and then it finally brightened up, so we had our picnic after all. It turned out to be a really nice afternoon.

Answer key: brighten up

  1. 1. D. The clouds will clear and the sky will get lighter
  2. 2. brightened up
  3. 3. A. Yes, I heard it's going to brighten up on Saturday.
  4. 4. Example: Last summer we planned a picnic in the park, but it was grey and drizzly all morning. We waited in a café for an hour, and then it finally brightened up, so we had our picnic after all. It turned out to be a really nice afternoon.
