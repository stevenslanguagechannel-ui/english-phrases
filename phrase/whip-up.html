whip up

Phrasal verbLevel B2CasualEveryday life

To make a meal quickly and easily, often with what you already have.

I'm starving, so I'll whip up some pasta.

Tip: Use 'whip up' + a meal or snack (whip up dinner, whip up a quick salad); it suggests speed and little effort, so avoid it for long, complicated cooking.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraAre you free to come over after work? I can't face a restaurant tonight.
KofiSure. Are you going to order something?
AmiraNo, there's no need. I'll whip up an omelette with whatever's in the fridge.
KofiPerfect. I'll bring some bread.
AmiraGreat. It'll be ready in ten minutes, so don't be late.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had to prepare a meal quickly. What did you make?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month some friends turned up without warning, so I had to whip up something fast. I had rice, a few eggs and some vegetables, so I made a simple fried rice. It wasn't fancy, but everyone enjoyed it and it was ready in about fifteen minutes.

Answer key: whip up

  1. 1. D. Make food quickly and without much fuss
  2. 2. whipped up
  3. 3. C. Why not whip up some eggs on toast? It takes five minutes.
  4. 4. Example: Last month some friends turned up without warning, so I had to whip up something fast. I had rice, a few eggs and some vegetables, so I made a simple fried rice. It wasn't fancy, but everyone enjoyed it and it was ready in about fifteen minutes.
