stand someone up

Phrasal verbLevel B2CasualPeople & relationships

To not come to a meeting you agreed to, without telling the other person.

She stood me up and I waited an hour at the café.

Tip: Use it as stand + person + up ("he stood me up"), because the person goes in the middle and not after "up". It's usually said about a date or a planned meeting where the other person never turned up and never called.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraYou seem a bit down. Did dinner with your cousin go okay?
KofiThere was no dinner. He stood me up again.
AmiraOh no. Did he at least text you?
KofiNothing. I sat in the restaurant for forty minutes before I gave up.
AmiraThat's really rude. I'd tell him how it made you feel.

4. Your turn: speak

Have you ever been kept waiting by someone who didn't turn up? What happened, and how did you feel?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Yes, once a friend stood me up outside the cinema. I waited for about thirty minutes and kept checking my phone, and I felt annoyed and a bit embarrassed. She apologised the next day, but I was still upset because she hadn't warned me.

Answer key: stand someone up

  1. 1. B. Didn't come to the meeting they had arranged, and didn't tell him
  2. 2. stood me up
  3. 3. A. No, she stood me up and didn't even message.
  4. 4. Example: Yes, once a friend stood me up outside the cinema. I waited for about thirty minutes and kept checking my phone, and I felt annoyed and a bit embarrassed. She apologised the next day, but I was still upset because she hadn't warned me.
