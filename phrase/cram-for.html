cram for

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualWork & study

To study a lot in a short time just before an exam.

I stayed in all weekend to cram for my English test.

Tip: Say 'cram for' + the exam or test (cram for my finals); it often suggests you left it late, and it's not used for steady, long-term study.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look exhausted, Kofi. Did you sleep at all?
KofiNot really. My anatomy exam is at nine tomorrow, and I only opened the textbook yesterday.
OlenaYesterday? Kofi, that's a huge amount of material.
KofiI know. I'm going to cram for it all evening with a pot of coffee.
OlenaOkay, but take a break at some point. I'll text you at eight to make sure you eat.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had to prepare for an exam or a big assessment quickly. What did you do?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I had a certification exam at work, and I didn't have much time because of a big project. I ended up having to cram for it over two evenings, mostly using flashcards and old practice papers. It worked, but I'd never leave it that late again.

Answer key: cram for

  1. 1. D. Study a lot in a short time just before an exam
  2. 2. cram for
  3. 3. B. Not really. I've been busy, so I'll have to cram for it on Thursday night.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I had a certification exam at work, and I didn't have much time because of a big project. I ended up having to cram for it over two evenings, mostly using flashcards and old practice papers. It worked, but I'd never leave it that late again.
