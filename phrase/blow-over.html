blow over

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralFeelings & reactions

To pass and end without causing serious trouble.

Don't worry. The argument will blow over by tomorrow.

Tip: Use 'blow over' for arguments, scandals, storms or awkward moments that fade without lasting damage; the subject is the problem, not a person (say 'the row will blow over', not 'she will blow over').

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you see Ken's face in the meeting? He was furious about the schedule change.
DiegoI did. Should I go and talk to him?
AmiraI'd give him a day. These things usually blow over once people have had time to cool down.
DiegoTrue. Last time he was upset, he was laughing about it by lunch.
AmiraExactly. Just don't bring it up again this afternoon.

2. Fill the gap

3. Choose the best reply

Olena: I'm so embarrassed. I sent that joke to the whole team by mistake.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a disagreement you had with someone close to you. How did it end?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few months ago I had a silly argument with my brother about who should organise our parents' anniversary dinner. We were both annoyed for a couple of days, but it blew over quickly once we sat down and talked. Now we just laugh about it.

Answer key: blow over

  1. 1. A. Fade away without causing serious or lasting trouble
  2. 2. blew over
  3. 3. C. Don't worry, it'll blow over. Everyone will forget it by Monday.
  4. 4. Example: A few months ago I had a silly argument with my brother about who should organise our parents' anniversary dinner. We were both annoyed for a couple of days, but it blew over quickly once we sat down and talked. Now we just laugh about it.
