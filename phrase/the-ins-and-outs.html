the ins and outs

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

The ins and outs are all the small details of how something works.

It took me a month to learn the ins and outs of my new job.

Tip: Almost always used as 'the ins and outs of + noun' after verbs like learn, know, understand or explain; it's plural, so don't say 'the in and out'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre you ready for your first shift at the bakery tomorrow?
KofiA bit nervous, honestly. I don't know the ins and outs of the ordering system yet.
OlenaDon't worry, nobody does on day one. Who's training you?
KofiAmira. She's been there for five years, so she'll show me everything.
OlenaPerfect. Just ask lots of questions and you'll be fine.

Show an example answer

I recently started using a new accounting program at work, and it was much more complicated than I thought. It took me a few weeks to understand the ins and outs of it, and I had to ask my colleagues for help a lot. Now I feel confident, and I can even show new people how to use it.
