crash at someone's place

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To sleep at someone's home for a night, usually in a casual way.

The party ends late, so you can crash at my place.

Tip: Say 'crash at my place' or 'crash at Ana's', never 'crash my place', and use it for a casual, one-night stay, not a planned visit.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaThe concert finishes at eleven, but my last bus leaves at half ten.
KofiDon't worry. You can crash at my place if it gets too late.
OlenaAre you sure? I don't want to be in the way.
KofiNot at all. I've got a spare mattress, and I'll make breakfast.
OlenaThat's really kind. I'll bring the coffee.

4. Your turn: speak

Have you ever stayed overnight at a friend's home? Tell me about it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Yes, last winter I missed the last bus after a study session, so I crashed at my friend Ken's place. He gave me a blanket and a spare pillow, and we had toast together in the morning. It was much easier than paying for a taxi home.

Answer key: crash at someone's place

  1. 1. A. Stay the night at my home in a relaxed, informal way
  2. 2. crash at my place
  3. 3. A. Oh no! Come and crash at my place tonight. I've got a sofa.
  4. 4. Example: Yes, last winter I missed the last bus after a study session, so I crashed at my friend Ken's place. He gave me a blanket and a spare pillow, and we had toast together in the morning. It was much easier than paying for a taxi home.
