crack up

Phrasal verbLevel B2CasualFeelings & reactions

To suddenly laugh a lot.

Ken told a joke and we all cracked up.

Tip: Use it for people (or a group) who laugh suddenly, as in 'I cracked up when…'; don't use it for being slightly amused, and remember the separable form 'crack someone up' means to make them laugh.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraHow was the team meeting? I heard it ran late.
DiegoIt did, but it was fun. Olena's video froze on a really odd face, and she didn't realise.
AmiraOh no! What did everyone do?
DiegoHonestly, the whole room cracked up, even our manager.
AmiraI wish I'd been there. Did she see it afterwards?
DiegoYes, and she laughed harder than anyone.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you couldn't stop laughing with other people. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year, my friends and I were trying to follow a very serious cooking video, and my friend dropped an egg on her own shoe. We all cracked up and couldn't stop for about five minutes. It's one of those silly moments I still remember.

Answer key: crack up

  1. 1. A. Started laughing a lot, suddenly
  2. 2. crack up
  3. 3. C. Yes, I cracked up! He looked so confused.
  4. 4. Example: Last year, my friends and I were trying to follow a very serious cooking video, and my friend dropped an egg on her own shoe. We all cracked up and couldn't stop for about five minutes. It's one of those silly moments I still remember.
