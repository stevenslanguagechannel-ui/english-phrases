slow on the uptake

IdiomLevel C1CasualWork & study

Slow to understand something or to catch on.

Sorry, I'm a bit slow on the uptake before my morning coffee.

Tip: Use it with 'be' or 'a bit/a little/rather' to describe a person, often as a gentle excuse for yourself; saying it about someone else can sound rude, so soften it or use it lightly.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaWait, so the meeting moved from Thursday to Friday, or the other way round?
KofiFriday to Thursday. I put it in the group chat twice this morning.
OlenaSorry, I've been slow on the uptake all day. I haven't slept much this week.
KofiNo worries. I'll send you a calendar invite so it's easy to find.
OlenaThanks, that'd help. I'll accept it right away.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you found it hard to understand something at first. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I started my new job, I was quite slow on the uptake with the booking software. Everyone else seemed to understand it right away, but it took me about a week to see how the different parts connected. After that, it felt much easier, and I even helped a new colleague learn it.

Answer key: slow on the uptake

  1. 1. A. Slow to understand or notice something
  2. 2. slow on the uptake
  3. 3. B. Don't worry, I was slow on the uptake with it too, until someone showed me.
  4. 4. Example: When I started my new job, I was quite slow on the uptake with the booking software. Everyone else seemed to understand it right away, but it took me about a week to see how the different parts connected. After that, it felt much easier, and I even helped a new colleague learn it.
