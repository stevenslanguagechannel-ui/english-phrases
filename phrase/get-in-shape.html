get in shape

ExpressionLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

To become healthy and strong by exercising.

I want to get in shape before the summer.

Tip: Use it as a verb phrase ('get in shape', 'trying to get in shape'); the opposite is 'be out of shape', and don't confuse it with 'in good shape', which can describe things as well as people.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraYou're up early, Ken. Are you going for a run?
KenYes, I signed up for a ten-kilometre race in March, so I need to get in shape.
AmiraTen kilometres! How far can you run now?
KenAbout two, and then I have to stop and catch my breath.
AmiraWell, you've got four months. I could join you on Saturdays if you like.
KenYes, please. I'd really like the company.

4. Your turn: speak

Have you ever tried to get fitter or start a new exercise habit? Tell me about it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I decided to get in shape, so I started cycling to work three times a week. It was hard at first because I got tired very quickly, but after a couple of months I felt much stronger and more energetic. Now I actually miss it when the weather is too bad to ride.

Answer key: get in shape

  1. 1. B. Become fit and strong through regular exercise
  2. 2. get in shape
  3. 3. C. You should get in shape. Maybe start with a short walk every evening?
  4. 4. Example: Last year I decided to get in shape, so I started cycling to work three times a week. It was hard at first because I got tired very quickly, but after a couple of months I felt much stronger and more energetic. Now I actually miss it when the weather is too bad to ride.
