the light bulb goes off

IdiomLevel B1CasualProblems & solutions

You suddenly understand something or get a good idea.

I read the instructions twice, and then the light bulb went off.

Tip: Use it for a sudden moment of understanding, usually in the past tense: 'the light bulb went off'; add 'for me' or 'when I saw...' to say what triggered it.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you ever fix the Wi-Fi at your place?
DiegoFinally, yes. I kept restarting the router and nothing worked.
OlenaSo what changed?
DiegoMy neighbour mentioned her network name was almost the same as mine. Then the light bulb went off. I'd been connecting to hers the whole time.
OlenaHa! No wonder it was so slow.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you were stuck on a problem and then suddenly understood how to solve it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I was learning to use a new accounting program at work, and nothing made sense for days. Then a colleague showed me one shortcut, and the light bulb went off. After that, I finished in an hour what had taken me a whole afternoon.

Answer key: the light bulb goes off

  1. 1. D. Diego suddenly understood what the problem was.
  2. 2. light bulb went off
  3. 3. B. Ah, so the light bulb went off, and now you know what to do differently.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I was learning to use a new accounting program at work, and nothing made sense for days. Then a colleague showed me one shortcut, and the light bulb went off. After that, I finished in an hour what had taken me a whole afternoon.
