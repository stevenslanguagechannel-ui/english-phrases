wrap up

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralWork & study

To finish something, such as a meeting or a task.

Let's wrap up the meeting so everyone can have lunch.

Tip: Use 'wrap up' with a thing as the object (wrap up a call, wrap up the project); it can go before or after a pronoun, as in 'wrap it up', but never 'wrap up it'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre you still on the budget report, Kofi?
KofiAlmost. I just need ten more minutes to wrap up the last section.
OlenaNo rush. The meeting with the client isn't until three.
KofiGood. I'll send it to you before lunch so you can check the numbers.
OlenaPerfect, thanks.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to finish a project or task under time pressure. How did you manage it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I had to finish a group presentation for my course in just two days. We split the slides between us, and on the last evening we met online to wrap up the final details. It was stressful, but we handed it in on time and got good feedback.

Answer key: wrap up

  1. 1. D. Finish the final part of something
  2. 2. wrap up
  3. 3. A. Great, let's wrap up and send everyone the notes.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I had to finish a group presentation for my course in just two days. We split the slides between us, and on the last evening we met online to wrap up the final details. It was stressful, but we handed it in on time and got good feedback.
