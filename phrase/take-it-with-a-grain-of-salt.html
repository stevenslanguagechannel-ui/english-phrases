take it with a grain of salt

IdiomLevel B2NeutralOpinions & honesty

Don't believe something completely, because it may not be fully true.

Online reviews can help, but I always take them with a grain of salt.

Tip: Use it as take + something + with a grain of salt, usually for news, rumours, reviews or claims; you can also say 'with a pinch of salt' in British English.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraKen, did you see that article? It says this new app can teach you Spanish in two weeks.
KenI saw it. It was written by the company that makes the app, though.
AmiraTrue. The testimonials look amazing, but they might be picked carefully.
KenExactly. I'd take all of that with a grain of salt.
AmiraMaybe I'll try the free week first and see for myself.
KenGood idea. Then you'll know what it really does.

Show an example answer

Not really. I enjoy it, but I take most posts with a grain of salt, especially dramatic news or health tips. I usually check a second source before I believe or share anything.

Answer key: take it with a grain of salt

  1. 1. A. Don't trust it completely, because it may be exaggerated
  2. 2. with a grain of salt
  3. 3. A. Maybe, but I'd take that with a grain of salt until I try it.
  4. 4. Example: Not really. I enjoy it, but I take most posts with a grain of salt, especially dramatic news or health tips. I usually check a second source before I believe or share anything.
