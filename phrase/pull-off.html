pull off

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralSuccess & progress

To succeed at something difficult.

She planned the whole wedding in a month and pulled it off.

Tip: Use it with an object: 'pull it off' (the pronoun goes in the middle), or 'pull off a big project'; it often follows 'manage to' or 'hope to' and suggests the task was risky or hard.

Hear it in conversation

KenDid your team finish the product demo for the client?
AmiraYes! The software crashed an hour before, and we had to rebuild the slides from scratch.
KenWow, that sounds stressful. Did the client notice anything?
AmiraNot at all. Honestly, I'm amazed we pulled it off.
KenYou must be exhausted. Let's get lunch, my treat.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had to do something difficult in a very short time. How did it go?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I had to organise a surprise party for my sister in only three days, and she kept asking questions. It was really stressful, but my friends helped, and we managed to pull it off. She was completely shocked when she walked in.

Answer key: pull off

  1. 1. A. Succeeded at something difficult
  2. 2. pulled it off
  3. 3. C. It was hard work, but I pulled it off, and everyone loved it.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I had to organise a surprise party for my sister in only three days, and she kept asking questions. It was really stressful, but my friends helped, and we managed to pull it off. She was completely shocked when she walked in.
