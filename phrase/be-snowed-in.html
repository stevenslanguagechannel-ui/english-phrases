be snowed in

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

To be unable to leave a place because there is too much snow outside.

We were snowed in all weekend, so we watched movies.

Tip: Use it with 'be' or 'get' (we were snowed in, we got snowed in); it is also used for a place, e.g. 'the village was snowed in', and don't confuse it with 'snowed under', which means having too much work.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHi Kofi, are you still coming to the cabin on Saturday?
KofiI'm not sure. The forecast says heavy snow all night, and the road up to you is steep.
OlenaCome on Friday, then. Last winter we got snowed in for two days and had to ration the coffee.
KofiTwo days! I'd need to tell my manager I might miss Monday.
OlenaShe'll understand. Just bring a good book and some warm socks.

Tell me about a time when bad weather changed your plans.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last January we planned a weekend trip to see my cousins, but it snowed all night and we woke up snowed in. We couldn't open the front door properly, let alone drive anywhere. In the end we stayed home, cooked a big soup, and had a video call with the family instead.

Answer key: be snowed in

  1. 1. D. Could not leave the cabin because of the heavy snow outside
  2. 2. snowed in
  3. 3. B. No, we were snowed in, so I couldn't even get the car out of the driveway.
  4. 4. Example: Last January we planned a weekend trip to see my cousins, but it snowed all night and we woke up snowed in. We couldn't open the front door properly, let alone drive anywhere. In the end we stayed home, cooked a big soup, and had a video call with the family instead.
