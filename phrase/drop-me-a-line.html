drop me a line

ExpressionLevel B2CasualEveryday life

To send someone a short message or email.

Drop me a line when you get home, so I know you're safe.

Tip: Use 'drop someone a line' (or 'drop me a line') as a friendly request or promise to get in touch briefly; it works for emails and texts, and it's more relaxed than 'contact me' or 'send me a report'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaI'm flying out to Lisbon tomorrow morning. I'll be there for two weeks.
KofiLovely! Are you going to have time to meet up with anyone?
OlenaMy cousin lives there, so yes. I'm not sure about the rest of the trip yet.
KofiWell, if you find a good café, drop me a line. I'm planning a trip there next spring.
OlenaGood idea. I'll send you a message with the name and a photo of the menu.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: I'm moving to Toronto next month. I'd love to stay in touch.

4. Your turn: speak

How do you usually keep in touch with friends who live in a different city or country?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I'm not great at long phone calls, so I mostly send short messages and photos. If a friend is going through something difficult, I'll say, 'Drop me a line whenever you want to talk.' That way they know I'm there without any pressure to reply right away.
