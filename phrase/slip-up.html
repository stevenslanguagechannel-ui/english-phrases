slip up

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To make a small mistake, often by not paying attention.

I slipped up and called my teacher 'Mum'.

Tip: Use it as 'slip up' (no object) or 'slip up on/with something'; it sounds lighter and less serious than 'fail', so it suits small, fixable mistakes.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you send the invoice to the right client this morning?
KofiNot exactly. I slipped up and used last month's email address.
OlenaOh no. Did they get it?
KofiNo, it bounced straight back. I've already resent it to the correct one.
OlenaGood. Easy mistake when you're rushing.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you made a small mistake that you had to fix quickly.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I slipped up when I was organising a meeting with friends. I put the wrong time in the invitation, so half of them arrived an hour late. I apologised and now I always check details twice before I send anything.

Answer key: slip up

  1. 1. A. Made a small mistake by not paying enough attention
  2. 2. slipped up
  3. 3. C. Don't worry, everyone slips up sometimes. Can you change the ticket?
  4. 4. Example: Last month I slipped up when I was organising a meeting with friends. I put the wrong time in the invitation, so half of them arrived an hour late. I apologised and now I always check details twice before I send anything.
