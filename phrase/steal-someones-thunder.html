steal someone's thunder

IdiomLevel B2CasualWork & study

To take attention away from someone by doing or saying their big news first.

I wanted to announce the news, but Ken stole my thunder.

Tip: Use it as 'steal someone's thunder' (or 'steal the thunder'), and change the verb: 'stole my thunder', 'don't steal her thunder'. It's usually said after the event, as a mild complaint.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you hear? Amira got the team leader job!
DiegoI did, yes. I thought she was going to tell everyone at Friday's meeting.
OlenaShe was. But Kofi congratulated her in the group chat this morning.
DiegoOh no. He stole her thunder, didn't he?
OlenaHe did, though he didn't mean to. She looked a bit flat about it.
DiegoI'll make sure to make a fuss of her on Friday anyway.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I passed my driving test, and I wanted to surprise my parents at dinner. Unfortunately my brother saw the result on my phone and told them an hour before, so he completely stole my thunder. I was a bit disappointed, but we still celebrated together in the end.

Answer key: steal someone's thunder

  1. 1. A. Spoiled the moment by sharing her news before she could
  2. 2. stole her thunder
  3. 3. B. Oh no, he stole your thunder. Are you annoyed?
  4. 4. Example: Last year I passed my driving test, and I wanted to surprise my parents at dinner. Unfortunately my brother saw the result on my phone and told them an hour before, so he completely stole my thunder. I was a bit disappointed, but we still celebrated together in the end.
