get the hang of

IdiomLevel B1NeutralSuccess & progress

To slowly learn how to do something.

It was hard at first, but I'm getting the hang of the past tense.

Tip: Use it as 'get the hang of + noun' or 'get the hang of + -ing'; it often follows a first stage of struggling, and it's used for skills and routines, not facts.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHow's the new job going, Kofi?
KofiTiring! The scheduling software confused me all week.
OlenaOh no. Is it any better now?
KofiA bit. I think I'm starting to get the hang of it, though. Today I only needed help twice.
OlenaThat's progress. Give it another week and it'll feel easy.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about something you found difficult to learn at first but are better at now.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I found cooking really hard when I first moved out, and I burned a lot of dinners. After a few months I got the hang of it, and now I can make several dishes without a recipe.

Answer key: get the hang of

  1. 1. C. Start to understand how to do something and feel more confident
  2. 2. got the hang of
  3. 3. A. Don't worry, it took me a few tries to get the hang of it too.
  4. 4. Example: I found cooking really hard when I first moved out, and I burned a lot of dinners. After a few months I got the hang of it, and now I can make several dishes without a recipe.
