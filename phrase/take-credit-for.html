take credit for

ExpressionLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To say, or let people believe, that you did something good.

I only helped a little, so I can't take credit for the project.

Tip: Use it with a noun or -ing form: 'take credit for the idea' or 'take credit for fixing it'. It can be positive (you deserve it) or negative (you don't), so context and tone matter.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaThe client loved the new website design. Great job, Ken!
KenThanks, but Amira did most of the layout. I just fixed a few small things.
OlenaReally? Your manager told everyone it was your work.
KenThat's awkward. I don't want to take credit for something I barely touched.
OlenaThen tell her. Amira should hear her name in the meeting.
KenYou're right. I'll send an email before lunch.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when someone else deserved praise for something. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year my coworker stayed late for weeks to finish a big report, but our boss thanked me in the meeting. I didn't want to take credit for her hard work, so I told everyone she had done most of it. She looked really happy, and I felt much better.

Answer key: take credit for

  1. 1. D. To say or let people believe you did something good
  2. 2. take credit for
  3. 3. C. Not really. Diego did the planning, so I can't take credit for it.
