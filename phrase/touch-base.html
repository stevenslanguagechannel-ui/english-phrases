touch base

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To contact someone briefly to share news or check how things are going.

Let's touch base on Friday to see how the project is going.

Tip: Use it as a verb, often with 'with' + person or 'on' + topic: 'I'll touch base with Ken tomorrow.' It's common at work but also fine between friends; don't use it for a long, serious meeting.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHi Kofi, did you get the quote from the printing company?
KofiNot yet. They said they'd send it today, but nothing has arrived.
OlenaCould you touch base with them this afternoon? We need to order by Monday.
KofiSure. I'll give them a quick call after lunch.
OlenaThanks. Let me know what they say.

Tell me about a time when you needed to stay in contact with someone while working on something together.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I worked on a group project with two classmates who lived far away. We were all busy, so we agreed to touch base every Sunday evening with a short message or call. It kept everyone on track and nothing was forgotten.

Answer key: touch base

  1. 1. A. Contact them briefly to check on progress
  2. 2. touch base
  3. 3. C. Probably, but I'll touch base with everyone next month once the dates are clear.
