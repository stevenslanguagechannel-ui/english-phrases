get around to

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralTime & effort

To finally do something you meant to do for a long time.

I finally got around to cleaning the garage this weekend.

Tip: Use 'get around to' + noun or -ing (get around to the report / get around to calling her); it often appears in negatives like 'I haven't got around to it yet', which suggests you've been delaying.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you ever send that parcel to your sister?
KofiNot yet. It's been sitting by my front door for two weeks.
OlenaTwo weeks! Her birthday was last Friday, wasn't it?
KofiI know, I know. I just haven't got around to going to the post office.
OlenaIt closes at five. Go on your lunch break tomorrow.
KofiOkay, okay. I'll do it first thing, before I forget again.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about something you meant to do for a long time but finally did.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

For almost a year I wanted to organise all my photos, but there were thousands of them on my phone. Last month I finally got around to it during a rainy weekend. It took a few hours, and I felt so relieved afterwards.

Answer key: get around to

  1. 1. A. Haven't found the time or motivation to go, though I meant to
  2. 2. got around to fixing
  3. 3. B. Sorry, not yet. I keep meaning to, but I haven't got around to it.
  4. 4. Example: For almost a year I wanted to organise all my photos, but there were thousands of them on my phone. Last month I finally got around to it during a rainy weekend. It took a few hours, and I felt so relieved afterwards.
