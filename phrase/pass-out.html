pass out

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To fall asleep suddenly because you are very tired, or to faint.

I was so tired after the trip that I passed out on the sofa.

Tip: Use 'pass out' as a phrasal verb with no object (someone passes out); it can mean fainting or falling asleep suddenly, so context shows which one you mean.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you get to the end of the film last night?
KofiNo, I passed out about twenty minutes in. I'd been up since five.
AmiraHa! You missed the best part.
KofiDon't tell me what happens. I'll watch it again tonight.
AmiraOkay, but try to stay awake this time.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I worked two night shifts in a row and then took a long train home. I was so exhausted that I passed out as soon as I sat down and nearly missed my station. Since then I try to get proper sleep before any long journey.

Answer key: pass out

  1. 1. D. Fell asleep suddenly because he was very tired
  2. 2. passed out
  3. 3. A. Not really. I skipped lunch and I think I'm going to pass out.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I worked two night shifts in a row and then took a long train home. I was so exhausted that I passed out as soon as I sat down and nearly missed my station. Since then I try to get proper sleep before any long journey.
