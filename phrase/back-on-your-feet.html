back on your feet

IdiomLevel B2NeutralEveryday life

Healthy and active again after being ill or having problems.

Rest this week and you'll be back on your feet soon.

Tip: Use it after 'be' or 'get' (be back on your feet, get back on your feet) for people recovering from illness, injury or a hard period; it can also describe a business or a person's finances recovering.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraIs Kofi still off work? I haven't seen him since the flu hit the office.
DiegoHe came in on Monday, but only for half a day. He looked pretty pale.
AmiraPoor guy. He was in bed for almost two weeks, wasn't he?
DiegoYes, but he says he's nearly back on your feet... sorry, back on his feet. He even went for a short walk yesterday.
AmiraGood. I'll bring him some soup tomorrow so he doesn't have to cook.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had a difficult period, such as an illness or a stressful time, and how you recovered.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A couple of years ago I had a bad back injury and couldn't work for about six weeks. My family helped a lot, and I did my exercises every day. Now I'm completely back on my feet and I try to look after my back much more carefully.

Answer key: back on your feet

  1. 1. A. Well enough to be active and normal again after being ill
  2. 2. back on his feet
  3. 3. C. She's doing much better. She's back on her feet and has started a new job.
  4. 4. Example: A couple of years ago I had a bad back injury and couldn't work for about six weeks. My family helped a lot, and I did my exercises every day. Now I'm completely back on my feet and I try to look after my back much more carefully.
