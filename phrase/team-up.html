team up

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralWork & study

To join someone and work together to do something.

Let's team up and cook dinner together tonight.

Tip: Use 'team up with someone' or 'team up to do something'; it often describes a choice to work together, so it's common in plans, projects and games.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHave you started the group presentation yet? I'm still stuck on the slides.
KofiNot really. I've got the research done, but my design skills are terrible.
OlenaWell, I'm good at design but I hate searching for data.
KofiThen why don't we team up? You make the slides, and I'll send you everything I found.
OlenaPerfect. Send me your notes tonight and I'll start tomorrow morning.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you worked with someone else to finish something difficult. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I had to move to a new apartment, and I had far too much to organise. I teamed up with my cousin, who is great at packing, while I handled the paperwork and the van. We finished in two days, which would have taken me a week alone.

Answer key: team up

  1. 1. B. Work together with someone to get something done
  2. 2. team up
  3. 3. B. Don't worry, let's team up and do it together.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I had to move to a new apartment, and I had far too much to organise. I teamed up with my cousin, who is great at packing, while I handled the paperwork and the van. We finished in two days, which would have taken me a week alone.
