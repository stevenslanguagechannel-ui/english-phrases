take for granted

IdiomLevel B2NeutralPeople & relationships

To take something for granted means to forget how lucky you are to have it, or to expect it will always be there.

I took my health for granted until I got sick last winter.

Tip: Use 'take (someone/something) for granted' for things or people you stop appreciating; the object can go in the middle ('take it for granted') or you can use 'take for granted that…' for assumptions.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraKen, thanks again for driving me to the airport at five in the morning.
KenNo problem. You'd do the same for me.
AmiraStill, I don't want you to think I take it for granted. It's a huge favour.
KenHonestly, I like hearing that. People usually just expect a lift.
AmiraWell, let me buy you breakfast when I'm back.
KenDeal. Text me when you land.

Show an example answer

I think a lot of people take clean running water for granted. When I travelled in a rural area last year, the supply kept cutting out, and I suddenly realised how much I depend on it. Now I try to be more thankful for the small things at home.

  2. 2. take for granted
  3. 3. A. You should be careful not to take her for granted.
