point blank

IdiomLevel B2NeutralOpinions & honesty

In a direct, straightforward way, without hesitating or being polite first.

I asked him point blank if he was coming to the party.

Tip: Use it with verbs of asking, telling or refusing (ask, tell, say, refuse point blank); it goes after the verb or object, and it's not used for describing a person's character.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you ever find out why the landlord kept putting off the repairs?
KofiYes, I got tired of the excuses, so I asked him point blank.
AmiraAnd what did he say?
KofiHe admitted he didn't have the money yet. At least I know where I stand now.
AmiraThat's better than waiting another month for an answer.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had to ask someone a difficult question directly.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few months ago my flatmate kept hinting that she might move out, and I couldn't plan my budget. In the end I asked her point blank whether she was staying. It felt awkward for a minute, but she said yes, and we both felt relieved.

Answer key: point blank

  1. 1. D. Directly, without hinting or being careful first
  2. 2. point blank
  3. 3. C. Just ask him point blank. Then he can't dodge it.
  4. 4. Example: A few months ago my flatmate kept hinting that she might move out, and I couldn't plan my budget. In the end I asked her point blank whether she was staying. It felt awkward for a minute, but she said yes, and we both felt relieved.
