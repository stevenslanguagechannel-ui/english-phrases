wear off

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralEveryday life

To slowly stop, like a feeling or the effect of medicine.

The numbness will wear off in about an hour.

Tip: Use it without an object: something 'wears off' by itself, and you can add 'after' or 'by' a time, as in 'wears off after a few hours'.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraAre you still feeling jet-lagged?
KofiA bit. I woke up at four this morning wide awake.
AmiraThat's rough. It should get easier soon, shouldn't it?
KofiYeah, I hope so. The tiredness only wore off around noon yesterday.
AmiraThen you'll probably be back to normal by the weekend.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when a strong feeling or effect lasted for a while and then slowly disappeared.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I ran my first half marathon, and I was so excited that I couldn't stop smiling for days. Then the feeling slowly wore off, and I went back to my normal routine. But I still think about that day whenever I need some motivation.

Answer key: wear off

  1. 1. D. Slowly became weaker and then stopped
  2. 2. wore off
  3. 3. A. Don't worry, the caffeine will wear off in a few hours.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I ran my first half marathon, and I was so excited that I couldn't stop smiling for days. Then the feeling slowly wore off, and I went back to my normal routine. But I still think about that day whenever I need some motivation.
