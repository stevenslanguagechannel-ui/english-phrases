end up + -ing

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To do something in the end, often without planning it.

We planned a quick lunch, but we ended up talking for three hours.

Tip: Use 'end up' + -ing for a result you didn't plan; the verb after it is always the -ing form, and the tense goes on 'end up' (ended up, will end up).

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you find a cheap flight to Lisbon in the end?
KofiNot really. The direct ones were too expensive, so I ended up taking a bus to Madrid first.
AmiraOh no, that sounds like a long day.
KofiIt was, but the tickets were half the price, so I can't complain.
AmiraTrue. Send me a photo when you get there!

Show an example answer

Last summer I planned to stay home and relax, but a friend invited me to a small town by the sea. We ended up spending the whole weekend there, and it was one of the best trips I've had.

Answer key: end up + -ing

  1. 1. B. He finally took a bus, even though he hadn't planned to at first.
  2. 2. ended up buying
  3. 3. B. We were tired, but the view was great, so we ended up walking to the top.
  4. 4. Example: Last summer I planned to stay home and relax, but a friend invited me to a small town by the sea. We ended up spending the whole weekend there, and it was one of the best trips I've had.
