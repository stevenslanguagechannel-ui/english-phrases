What's the worst that could happen?

ExpressionLevel B2CasualFeelings & reactions

A rhetorical question that means the risk is small, so it's fine to try.

Let's try the new restaurant. What's the worst that could happen?

Tip: Say it after you suggest an idea, to show you aren't worried; it's a question, so it often has no real answer, and it can sound careless if the risk is actually serious.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaI'm thinking of emailing the CEO about my idea for the new app.
KofiReally? Isn't that a bit risky? She doesn't know you.
OlenaMaybe, but she might love it. What's the worst that could happen?
KofiI guess she says no, or doesn't reply at all.
OlenaExactly. Then I'm in the same place I am now.
KofiFair enough. Send it, and tell me what she says.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you tried something new even though you weren't sure about it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I joined a local running club, even though I'm not very fast. I was nervous, but I thought, what's the worst that could happen? In the end, everyone was friendly, and now I run with them every weekend.

Answer key: What's the worst that could happen?

  1. 1. C. The risk is small, so it's fine to try.
  2. 2. What's the worst that could happen?
  3. 3. A. Just ask her politely. What's the worst that could happen?
  4. 4. Example: Last year I joined a local running club, even though I'm not very fast. I was nervous, but I thought, what's the worst that could happen? In the end, everyone was friendly, and now I run with them every weekend.
