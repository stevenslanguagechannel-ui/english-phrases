a stone's throw from

IdiomLevel B2NeutralEveryday life

Very close to a place.

My flat is just a stone's throw from the train station.

Tip: Use 'a stone's throw from' + a place (or 'a stone's throw away') to say somewhere is very near; it's about distance, not about throwing anything.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHave you found a hotel for the conference yet?
KofiYes, I booked one that's a stone's throw from the venue.
OlenaNice. So no taxis in the morning?
KofiExactly. I can leave ten minutes before it starts and still be early.
OlenaSmart. I'm forty minutes away, so I'll be setting an early alarm.

Answer key: a stone's throw from

  1. 1. C. Very close to
  2. 2. a stone's throw from
  3. 3. A. Not at all, it's a stone's throw from my front door.
  4. 4. Example: I'm lucky because everything I need is nearby. There's a small supermarket and a park just a stone's throw from my building, so I can do errands on foot. The only thing I have to travel for is work.
