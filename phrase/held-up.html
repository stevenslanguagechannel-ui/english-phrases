held up

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralProblems & solutions

To be delayed by something you cannot control.

Sorry I'm late. I got held up at security.

Tip: Use it in the passive ("I got held up" / "I was held up"), often with a place or cause: "held up in traffic", "held up at the border"; don't use it for people deliberately making you late with no outside cause, or for robberies unless the context is clear.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaKofi, where are you? The client's already here.
KofiI'm so sorry, Olena. The train stopped between stations and we were held up for twenty minutes.
OlenaOh no. Are you close now?
KofiFive minutes away. Can you start without me?
OlenaSure, I'll show them the first slides and keep them busy.
KofiThanks, you're a lifesaver.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "were held up" mean?

2. Fill the gap

We were meant to land at six, but we were on the runway for an hour because of fog.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: Why didn't you answer my calls this morning?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you were late for something important. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I was meant to meet a friend at the airport, but I got held up by roadworks on the motorway. The traffic barely moved for forty minutes, so I called her to explain. Luckily her flight was late too, so it didn't matter in the end.

Answer key: held up

  1. 1. C. Were delayed by something outside their control
  2. 2. held up
  3. 3. B. Sorry, I was held up in a meeting that ran over.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I was meant to meet a friend at the airport, but I got held up by roadworks on the motorway. The traffic barely moved for forty minutes, so I called her to explain. Luckily her flight was late too, so it didn't matter in the end.
