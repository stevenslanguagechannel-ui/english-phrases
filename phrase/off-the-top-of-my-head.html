off the top of my head

IdiomLevel B2CasualEveryday life

Said when you answer from memory, without checking or thinking for long.

Off the top of my head, I think the bus leaves at eight.

Tip: Put it at the start of a sentence with a comma, or at the end, to warn the listener that your answer is a quick guess and might not be exact.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDo you know how much a train ticket to Leeds costs these days?
KofiOff the top of my head, maybe forty pounds? But don't quote me.
OlenaThat's more than I expected. Can you check the website?
KofiSure. Oh, it's actually forty-six if you travel before ten.
OlenaThanks. Good thing I asked before I booked.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when someone asked you for information and you weren't completely sure of the answer.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A colleague once asked me how many people were coming to our team lunch. Off the top of my head, I said twelve, but I wasn't certain. I checked the list afterwards and it was actually fourteen, so I told the restaurant straight away.

Answer key: off the top of my head

  1. 1. B. Answering from memory, without checking, so it may not be exact
  2. 2. off the top of my head
  3. 3. B. Off the top of my head, I think it's five, but I'd check online.
  4. 4. Example: A colleague once asked me how many people were coming to our team lunch. Off the top of my head, I said twelve, but I wasn't certain. I checked the list afterwards and it was actually fourteen, so I told the restaurant straight away.
