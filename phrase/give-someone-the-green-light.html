give someone the green light

IdiomLevel B1NeutralWork & study

To give permission to start something.

The manager gave us the green light to start the project.

Tip: Use it as 'give someone the green light (to do something)' or 'get the green light'; it is common in work and planning, and the green light usually comes from someone with authority.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid Kofi reply about the new office layout?
AmiraYes! The director finally gave him the green light this morning.
OlenaGreat. So when can the builders start?
AmiraMonday, if the permits arrive on time.
OlenaThen I'll start packing up my desk today.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does 'gave him the green light' mean?

2. Fill the gap

We can't order the equipment until the board for the budget.

3. Choose the best reply

Ken: Have you heard anything about your holiday request yet?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had to wait for permission before starting something.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I wanted to organise a small English club at my university. I had to wait two weeks before the department gave me the green light, and I was quite nervous. When it finally came, I started inviting people the same day.

Answer key: give someone the green light

  1. 1. A. Allowed him to go ahead with the plan
  2. 2. gives us the green light
  3. 3. C. Not yet, but my manager said she'd give me the green light by Friday.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I wanted to organise a small English club at my university. I had to wait two weeks before the department gave me the green light, and I was quite nervous. When it finally came, I started inviting people the same day.
