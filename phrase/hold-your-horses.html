hold your horses

IdiomLevel B1CasualFeelings & reactions

You say 'hold your horses' to tell someone to wait or slow down.

Hold your horses! We don't have to run, the flight is not until six.

Tip: Say it as a command, usually with an exclamation, to someone who is rushing or jumping to conclusions; it is friendly between equals but can sound bossy to a manager or a stranger.

Hear it in conversation

KofiI've read the contract twice. Let's just sign it and send it back today.
OlenaHold your horses, Kofi. We haven't checked the cancellation terms yet.
KofiOh, you're right. I was in such a hurry to finish.
OlenaIt's only page nine. Give me ten minutes and we'll go through it together.
KofiFine. I'll make us some tea while you read.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you or someone you know made a decision too quickly. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

My brother once wanted to buy a second-hand car the first time he saw it. I said, 'Hold your horses, let's get a mechanic to look at it first.' It was lucky we waited, because the mechanic found a serious problem with the engine.

Answer key: hold your horses

  2. 2. hold your horses
  3. 3. A. Hold your horses! Check the size guide and the return policy first.
