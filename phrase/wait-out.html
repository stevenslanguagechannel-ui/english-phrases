wait out

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralEveryday life

To stay where you are until something bad or unpleasant is over.

Let's wait out the rain in the café before we walk home.

Tip: Use it with the bad thing as the object: 'wait out a storm / a delay / a bad patch'; it can split around a short object ('wait the storm out'), but not around a pronoun-less phrase like 'wait out for'.

Hear it in conversation

KofiOur flight's delayed again. They're saying another three hours because of the fog.
AmiraUgh. Should we try to get on a different flight?
KofiI checked, and everything else is full. I think we just have to wait it out.
AmiraFine. Let's find somewhere with decent chairs and a charger.
KofiGood idea. I'll get us some snacks, and we can watch a film on my laptop.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to be patient because something unpleasant was happening and you couldn't change it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last winter there was a huge storm while I was visiting my cousin, and all the roads were closed. We couldn't go anywhere, so we just waited it out at home, cooked a lot and played cards. It was boring at first, but it turned into a really nice day.

Answer key: wait out

  1. 1. C. Stay at the airport until the delay is over, because there's no better choice
  2. 2. wait it out
  3. 3. C. Honestly, I'd just wait out the storm at a hotel nearby and go home in the morning.
  4. 4. Example: Last winter there was a huge storm while I was visiting my cousin, and all the roads were closed. We couldn't go anywhere, so we just waited it out at home, cooked a lot and played cards. It was boring at first, but it turned into a really nice day.
