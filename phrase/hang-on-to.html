hang on to

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To keep something safe for someone, or to not give it away.

Can you hang on to my keys while I swim?

Tip: Use 'hang on to' + object (or 'hang on to it/them'); the object can also come before 'on to' only as a pronoun. Don't confuse it with 'hang on' (= wait) or 'hold on to' in the sense of 'keep tight grip', though both overlap.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraAre you coming to the pool with us after work?
KofiYes, but I can't take my laptop bag into the changing room.
AmiraNo problem. I'll leave my coat in a locker, but you could give the bag to Olena.
OlenaSure, I'll hang on to it at the café by the entrance until you're done.
KofiThanks, that's a huge help. I'll only be forty minutes.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you kept something safe for a friend or family member.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last summer my brother moved to a new city, and he asked me to hang on to his bicycle for a few months. I kept it in my hallway and made sure nobody borrowed it. When he came back in September, it was still in perfect condition.

Answer key: hang on to

  1. 1. A. Keep something safe for someone while they are busy
  2. 2. hang on to
  3. 3. A. Of course. I'll hang on to it until you're back.
  4. 4. Example: Last summer my brother moved to a new city, and he asked me to hang on to his bicycle for a few months. I kept it in my hallway and made sure nobody borrowed it. When he came back in September, it was still in perfect condition.
