bundle up

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To put on warm clothes because it is very cold.

Bundle up. It's below zero outside.

Tip: Use it as an instruction or advice ('Bundle up!') or with 'in' for the clothes: 'bundle up in a big coat'; don't use it for covering other things, like wrapping a gift.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre you walking to the station tonight?
KofiYeah, it's only fifteen minutes.
OlenaThe wind is really sharp now. You should bundle up.
KofiGood point. I'll find my scarf and my thick gloves.
OlenaAnd your hat! Your ears will freeze.
KofiOkay, okay. I'll look like a snowman.

4. Your turn: speak

How do you usually prepare when you have to go out in very cold weather?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When it's really cold, I always bundle up before I leave. I wear a thick coat, a scarf and gloves, and I put on an extra layer underneath. Last winter I forgot my hat once, and I never made that mistake again.

Answer key: bundle up

  1. 1. C. Put on plenty of warm clothes
  2. 2. bundle up
  3. 3. C. Then we should all bundle up. I'll wear my long coat.
