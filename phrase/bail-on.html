bail on

Phrasal verbLevel B2CasualPeople & relationships

To cancel a plan at the last minute, especially when others are counting on you.

I'm sorry I bailed on the picnic. I was so tired.

Tip: Use 'bail on' + a person, event or plan (bail on dinner, bail on me); it's casual and can sound a little critical, so avoid it in formal writing.

Hear it in conversation

KofiAre you coming to Olena's housewarming tonight?
AmiraI really want to, but I've had a awful week.
KofiShe's cooked for twelve people, though.
AmiraI know. I feel terrible, but I think I'm going to bail on her.
KofiThen text her now, not at seven o'clock.
AmiraGood point. I'll do it right away.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had to cancel a plan with friends or family at the last minute. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few months ago I had to bail on a weekend trip with some friends because I came down with a bad cold. I felt guilty because they'd already booked the hotel. I called them as soon as I knew, and they were very understanding about it.

Answer key: bail on

  1. 1. B. Cancel at the last minute on someone who is expecting you
  2. 2. bailing on
  3. 3. C. Sorry, I'm going to have to bail on you. My boss just gave me a deadline for tomorrow.
  4. 4. Example: A few months ago I had to bail on a weekend trip with some friends because I came down with a bad cold. I felt guilty because they'd already booked the hotel. I called them as soon as I knew, and they were very understanding about it.
