up in the air

IdiomLevel B2NeutralTime & effort

Not decided yet; still uncertain.

The party is still up in the air because we don't know the date.

Tip: Use it about plans, decisions or results that are still open: 'X is up in the air' or 'leave it up in the air'; it is not used for people, only for things that haven't been settled.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre you still coming to Lisbon with us in March?
KofiI'd love to, but it's all up in the air at the moment.
OlenaIs it a work thing?
KofiYes. My manager hasn't said whether the project will finish before then.
OlenaOkay. The flights are cheaper if we book this week, though.
KofiI know. I'll ask her tomorrow and tell you straight away.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a plan you made that changed or wasn't certain for a while. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last summer I planned a trip to see my cousin, but my work schedule was up in the air until the last minute. I couldn't book anything, which was stressful. In the end my manager approved my days off, and I managed to get a ticket just in time.

Answer key: up in the air

  1. 1. A. Not decided yet, so Kofi can't say for sure
  2. 2. up in the air
  3. 3. C. Not yet. It's still up in the air until the director gets back.
  4. 4. Example: Last summer I planned a trip to see my cousin, but my work schedule was up in the air until the last minute. I couldn't book anything, which was stressful. In the end my manager approved my days off, and I managed to get a ticket just in time.
