right as rain

IdiomLevel B2CasualEveryday life

Completely healthy and well again.

Take a rest and you'll be right as rain tomorrow.

Tip: Use it after 'be' or 'feel' (or 'will be') to say someone has recovered from a minor illness or problem; it is not used for serious conditions or for objects.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaKofi, you look so much better today. How's the stomach?
KofiMuch better, thanks. I stayed in bed all weekend and drank loads of water.
OlenaDo you think you'll be able to come to the team lunch on Friday?
KofiDefinitely. I'm right as rain now, I just need to eat something proper.
OlenaGreat. I'll book a table for six, then.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you were ill or very tired and then recovered quickly. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last winter I caught a bad cold just before an important presentation. I stayed home, slept a lot and drank hot tea, and after two days I was right as rain. I was really glad I didn't push myself to go in.

Answer key: right as rain

  1. 1. C. Feeling well again after being ill
  2. 2. right as rain
  3. 3. A. Yes, I woke up feeling right as rain.
  4. 4. Example: Last winter I caught a bad cold just before an important presentation. I stayed home, slept a lot and drank hot tea, and after two days I was right as rain. I was really glad I didn't push myself to go in.
