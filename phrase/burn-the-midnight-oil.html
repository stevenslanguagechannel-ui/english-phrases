burn the midnight oil

IdiomLevel B2CasualWork & study

To work or study very late at night.

I'm going to burn the midnight oil tonight to finish my project.

Tip: Use it as a verb phrase about yourself or others ('I'll be burning the midnight oil'); it means working late into the night, and it's often used for deadlines or exams, not for a normal late shift.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look exhausted, Kofi. Did you sleep at all?
KofiNot really. The report is due at nine, so I was burning the midnight oil until almost four.
OlenaFour! Is it finished now?
KofiNearly. I just need to check the numbers on the last page.
OlenaLet me get you a coffee. You've earned it.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "burning the midnight oil" mean?

2. Fill the gap

Amira has an exam on Monday, so she'll be all weekend.

3. Choose the best reply

Ken: The deadline is tomorrow morning and we're nowhere near done. What's the plan?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to work or study very hard to meet a deadline.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I had to finish a big presentation for work, and I left it too late. I ended up burning the midnight oil for three nights in a row, and I was really tired by the end. It went well, though, so I felt it was worth it.

Answer key: burn the midnight oil

  1. 1. A. Working late into the night to get something done
  2. 2. burning the midnight oil
  3. 3. C. I guess we'll be burning the midnight oil tonight.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I had to finish a big presentation for work, and I left it too late. I ended up burning the midnight oil for three nights in a row, and I was really tired by the end. It went well, though, so I felt it was worth it.
