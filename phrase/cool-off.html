cool off

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To become less hot, or to do something to feel less hot.

We went to the lake to cool off after the hot walk.

Tip: Use it for temperature ("cool off" in water or shade) and for emotions ("give him time to cool off"); the object goes in the middle: "cool him off", not "cool off him".

Hear it in conversation

AmiraIs the flat still boiling? I've been out all afternoon.
KofiYes, it's awful. I've had every window open since lunch.
AmiraLet's get some ice cream and sit in the park for a bit.
KofiGood idea. We can cool off under the trees before we go back.
AmiraAnd by then it might be cooler inside, too.

4. Your turn: speak

How do you usually deal with very hot weather? Tell me about a time when you had to find a way to cool off.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When it gets really hot, I usually stay inside until the evening. Last summer we had a heatwave, and my friends and I went to a river outside the city just to cool off. We sat in the water for hours, and honestly it was the best part of the whole week.

Answer key: cool off

  1. 1. B. Get less hot by finding a cooler place or doing something refreshing
  2. 2. cool off
  3. 3. A. Sit by the fan for a few minutes and cool off.
  4. 4. Example: When it gets really hot, I usually stay inside until the evening. Last summer we had a heatwave, and my friends and I went to a river outside the city just to cool off. We sat in the water for hours, and honestly it was the best part of the whole week.
