give it a go

ExpressionLevel B1CasualTime & effort

To try something, even if you aren't sure you will be good at it.

I've never played tennis, but I'll give it a go this weekend.

Tip: Use it as 'give it a go' or 'give X a go' (e.g. 'give sushi-making a go'); it's friendly and encouraging, so people often say it to others as a suggestion: 'Why not give it a go?'

Hear it in conversation

OlenaThere's a free pottery class at the community centre on Saturday.
KofiPottery? I'm terrible with my hands. I can barely draw a straight line.
OlenaThat's the point, it's for complete beginners. Why not give it a go?
KofiWhat if I make something awful?
OlenaThen you'll have a funny story. I'll come with you.
KofiOkay, fine. Put my name down.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about something new you tried recently even though you weren't sure you would enjoy it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month my friend suggested a climbing gym, and I was nervous because I'm not very strong. But I decided to give it a go, and I actually loved it. Now I go every weekend.

Answer key: give it a go

  1. 1. D. Try it, even though you may not be good at it
  2. 2. give it a go
  3. 3. C. Just give it a go. You can always change it later.
  4. 4. Example: Last month my friend suggested a climbing gym, and I was nervous because I'm not very strong. But I decided to give it a go, and I actually loved it. Now I go every weekend.
