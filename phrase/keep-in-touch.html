keep in touch

ExpressionLevel B1NeutralPeople & relationships

To stay in contact with someone by calling or messaging.

It was lovely to meet you. Let's keep in touch.

Tip: Use 'keep in touch (with someone)' after meeting, moving away or finishing a course; it's also a polite way to end a goodbye, and the noun form is 'be in touch' or 'lose touch' when contact stops.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaIs that your last day at the office, Kofi?
KofiYes, my flight to Accra is on Saturday. I'm going to miss this team.
OlenaWe'll miss you too! Send me a message when you land.
KofiI will. I really want to keep in touch with everyone, even from far away.
OlenaGood. We'll set up a group chat tonight so nobody gets forgotten.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does 'keep in touch' mean?

2. Fill the gap

Even though Amira moved abroad for university, she and her old school friends still by video call every month.

3. Choose the best reply

Mei: It was so good to see you again after all these years. I don't want us to disappear on each other again.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a friend you met while travelling or studying. How do you stay connected with them now?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I met my friend Diego on a language course in Valencia a few years ago. We live in different countries now, but we really make an effort to keep in touch, mostly through voice messages and a video call every few weeks. It's easy to lose touch with people, so I think a small regular chat really helps.

Answer key: keep in touch

  1. 1. B. Stay in contact by messaging or calling, even when far apart
  2. 2. keep in touch
  3. 3. C. Me neither. Let's keep in touch this time, and not wait another five years.
