give someone a shout

ExpressionLevel B2CasualEveryday life

To contact someone, usually by phone or message, to tell them something.

Give me a shout when you get to the station.

Tip: Use 'give someone a shout' with a object pronoun or name, usually followed by 'when' or 'if'; it is common in British English and understood in American English, but 'give someone a call' or 'text someone' are safer in formal situations.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaI'm heading out of the office now. Are you still at the gym?
KofiYeah, I'll be done in about twenty minutes.
OlenaNo problem. I'm going to grab some groceries first.
KofiOkay. Give me a shout when you're back, and I'll come over to help with the bags.
OlenaPerfect, I'll message you as soon as I park.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you arranged to meet a friend or family member in a busy place. How did you find each other?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I met my cousin at a huge train station, and it was really crowded. I told her to give me a shout when she got off her train, and then we found each other near the main entrance. It was much easier than trying to guess where she was.

Answer key: give someone a shout

  1. 1. C. Contact me, for example by phone or message, to let me know
  2. 2. give you a shout
  3. 3. C. Don't worry, just give me a shout when you're close and I'll buy the tickets.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I met my cousin at a huge train station, and it was really crowded. I told her to give me a shout when she got off her train, and then we found each other near the main entrance. It was much easier than trying to guess where she was.
