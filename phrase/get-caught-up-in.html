get caught up in

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralTime & effort

To get caught up in something is to become so busy with it that you forget other things.

I got caught up in a TV show and forgot to cook dinner.

Tip: Use it with 'in' plus a noun or -ing form (get caught up in a conversation / in planning the trip); it often explains why you lost track of time or forgot something, and don't confuse it with 'be caught' meaning trapped or arrested.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraKen, I waited for you at the station for twenty minutes. Where were you?
KenI'm so sorry! I got caught up in a work call and completely lost track of time.
AmiraI wish you'd texted me. I thought something had happened.
KenYou're right. My phone was on silent, too. Have you eaten?
AmiraNot yet. Let's just go to that noodle place by the exit.

Show an example answer

Last weekend I started tidying my bookshelf, and I got caught up in sorting old photos I found. Before I knew it, three hours had passed and I'd missed lunch. It was fun, though, so I didn't mind.

Answer key: get caught up in

  1. 1. D. Became so involved in something that he forgot about other things
  2. 2. got caught up in
  3. 3. B. Sorry, I got caught up in a few meetings and forgot all about it.
  4. 4. Example: Last weekend I started tidying my bookshelf, and I got caught up in sorting old photos I found. Before I knew it, three hours had passed and I'd missed lunch. It was fun, though, so I didn't mind.
