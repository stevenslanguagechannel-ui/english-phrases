chalk it up to

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralProblems & solutions

To chalk something up to a reason means to say that reason explains it, often a mistake or bad luck.

I was late for the meeting. Let's chalk it up to the traffic.

Tip: Use the pattern 'chalk it up to + reason' (or 'chalk X up to Y'); the reason is often a noun or an -ing phrase, as in 'chalk it up to bad luck'.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraDid you fix the printer in the end?
KenNo. It printed half a page, then it just stopped.
AmiraAgain? That's the third time this week.
KenI know. I can't find anything wrong with it, so I'll chalk it up to the cold weather and try again tomorrow.
AmiraFine, but if it happens again, we're calling the repair company.

Workbook

2. Fill the gap

3. Choose the best reply

Olena: I got a really low mark on that quiz, and I studied for hours!

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time something went wrong and you couldn't find the reason. How did you deal with it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month my train was cancelled twice in one week, and nobody could explain why. In the end I decided to chalk it up to bad luck and leave earlier, which stopped me from worrying about it.

Answer key: chalk it up to

  1. 1. C. Decide the cold weather is the likely reason for the problem
  2. 2. chalk it up to
  3. 3. B. Don't worry. Just chalk it up to a bad day and move on.
  4. 4. Example: Last month my train was cancelled twice in one week, and nobody could explain why. In the end I decided to chalk it up to bad luck and leave earlier, which stopped me from worrying about it.
