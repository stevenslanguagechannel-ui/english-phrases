go to town

IdiomLevel B2CasualTime & effort

To do something with great energy and enthusiasm, without holding back.

The kids went to town decorating the cake with sprinkles.

Tip: Use it as 'go to town on/with something' or 'go to town doing something'; it's informal, usually positive, and describes enthusiastic effort rather than literally travelling.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYour flat looks amazing! Did you hire someone to do the garden?
DiegoNo, I did it all myself over the weekend. Once I started, I really went to town.
OlenaI can tell. Where did all those lights and flower pots come from?
DiegoThe garden centre had a sale, so I bought way more than I needed.
OlenaWell, it was worth it. It looks like a little café out there.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you put a lot of effort into a project or a celebration.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I organised a surprise birthday party for my sister, and I really went to town. I made decorations by hand, cooked for twenty people and even created a photo slideshow. It was exhausting, but seeing her face made it all worthwhile.

Answer key: go to town

  1. 1. C. Put in a lot of energy and effort, holding nothing back
  2. 2. went to town
  3. 3. B. Wow, she really went to town on it!
  4. 4. Example: Last year I organised a surprise birthday party for my sister, and I really went to town. I made decorations by hand, cooked for twenty people and even created a photo slideshow. It was exhausting, but seeing her face made it all worthwhile.
