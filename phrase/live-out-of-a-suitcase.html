live out of a suitcase

IdiomLevel B2CasualEveryday life

If you live out of a suitcase, you travel so much that you keep your clothes in your bag.

I've been living out of a suitcase for three weeks.

Tip: Use 'live out of a suitcase' (often with 'for' + a time period) to describe a stretch of constant travel or temporary living; it suggests you have no settled base, and it's usually a mild complaint or a tired joke.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHi Kofi! Are you back home for good now?
KofiOnly until Monday. This month I've been in four cities for work, and I'm tired of living out of a suitcase.
OlenaThat sounds exhausting. Have you at least unpacked this time?
KofiNot yet. I'm doing laundry first, then I'm repacking for Lisbon.
OlenaWell, tonight I'm cooking. You deserve a proper meal.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does 'living out of a suitcase' mean?

2. Fill the gap

After a year of constant business trips, Amira was ready to stop and have a real home base again.

3. Choose the best reply

Ken: I've been on the road since early October, and I haven't been home once.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to travel a lot or stay away from home for a while. How did you feel about it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few years ago I had a work project in three different countries, so I spent two months living out of a suitcase. At first it felt exciting, but after a few weeks I really missed my own bed and cooking my own food. Now I enjoy travelling, but I always like to have a real home to come back to.

Answer key: live out of a suitcase

  1. 1. A. Travelling so much that you never fully unpack or settle anywhere
  2. 2. living out of a suitcase
  3. 3. A. Wow, you must be living out of a suitcase by now!
  4. 4. Example: A few years ago I had a work project in three different countries, so I spent two months living out of a suitcase. At first it felt exciting, but after a few weeks I really missed my own bed and cooking my own food. Now I enjoy travelling, but I always like to have a real home to come back to.
