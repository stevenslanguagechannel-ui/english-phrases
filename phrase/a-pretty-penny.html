a pretty penny

IdiomLevel B2CasualEveryday life

A large amount of money.

That new phone must have cost a pretty penny.

Tip: Use it after verbs like cost, pay or fetch ('cost a pretty penny', 'paid a pretty penny'); it describes a high price, not a specific amount, and is not used for small sums.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraHave you seen Olena's new flat? The kitchen is incredible.
KofiI went last weekend. The whole place looks like a magazine photo.
AmiraThose worktops look like real stone. They must have cost a pretty penny.
KofiI think she saved for two years just for the renovation.
AmiraWell, it was worth it. I'd happily cook in that kitchen every day.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "a pretty penny" mean?

2. Fill the gap

My brother's wedding in Lisbon was lovely, but the flights and hotel cost us .

3. Choose the best reply

Ken: Did you see the price of those concert tickets? They're from the front rows.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about something you bought that turned out to be more expensive than you expected.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I bought a laptop for my studies, and I didn't realise how much the extra software would add up. In the end it cost me a pretty penny, but I use it every day, so I don't regret it.

Answer key: a pretty penny

  1. 1. C. A lot of money
  2. 2. a pretty penny
  3. 3. B. Yes, I'm sure they cost a pretty penny.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I bought a laptop for my studies, and I didn't realise how much the extra software would add up. In the end it cost me a pretty penny, but I use it every day, so I don't regret it.
