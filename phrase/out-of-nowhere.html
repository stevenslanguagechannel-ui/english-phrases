out of nowhere

IdiomLevel B1CasualFeelings & reactions

Suddenly and unexpectedly, with no warning.

A cat jumped out of nowhere and scared me.

Tip: Put it at the end or start of a clause: "came out of nowhere", "appeared out of nowhere". It often follows verbs like come, appear, hit, or show up. It works for things (rain, noise, news) and people.

Hear it in conversation

KenWhy are you late? I thought you were right behind me.
OlenaSorry! The traffic was fine, but then it started pouring out of nowhere.
KenOh no. Did you get wet?
OlenaCompletely. I didn't have an umbrella, so I waited in a shop for twenty minutes.
KenWell, at least you're here now. Let's get you a hot drink.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time something unexpected happened to you. What was it, and how did you react?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last summer I was walking to work when a huge storm started out of nowhere. The sky had been completely blue, so I wasn't ready at all. I ran into a café and waited there, and honestly I just laughed because it was so sudden.

Answer key: out of nowhere

  1. 1. A. Suddenly, without any warning
  2. 2. out of nowhere
  3. 3. A. No, it came out of nowhere. She never said a word.
  4. 4. Example: Last summer I was walking to work when a huge storm started out of nowhere. The sky had been completely blue, so I wasn't ready at all. I ran into a café and waited there, and honestly I just laughed because it was so sudden.
