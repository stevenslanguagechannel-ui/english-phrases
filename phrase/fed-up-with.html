fed up with

ExpressionLevel B1CasualFeelings & reactions

Annoyed and tired of something that keeps happening.

I'm fed up with washing the dishes every night.

Tip: Use 'fed up with' + a noun or -ing form (fed up with the noise, fed up with waiting); it's common in speech but a bit too casual for formal writing.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look tired, Kofi. Was the train late again?
KofiForty minutes this time. That's the third time this week.
OlenaHave you complained to the rail company?
KofiTwice. I'm so fed up with their excuses that I'm thinking of cycling to work.
OlenaIn this weather? Honestly, I wouldn't blame you.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does 'fed up with' mean?

2. Fill the gap

Amira is the noise from the building site next door, so she's started wearing earplugs at home.

3. Choose the best reply

Ken: I've been waiting on hold for an hour, and now the call has dropped again!

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about something in your daily life that you're fed up with, and what you do about it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I'm really fed up with the traffic on my way to work, because it makes the journey twice as long as it should be. To cope with it, I now leave earlier and listen to audiobooks, which makes the time feel more useful.

Answer key: fed up with

  1. 1. B. Annoyed and tired of something that keeps happening
  2. 2. fed up with
  3. 3. C. Oh no, I'd be fed up with that too.
  4. 4. Example: I'm really fed up with the traffic on my way to work, because it makes the journey twice as long as it should be. To cope with it, I now leave earlier and listen to audiobooks, which makes the time feel more useful.
