come down to

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralProblems & solutions

To be the most important reason or factor in a decision or situation.

Choosing a flat really comes down to price and location.

Tip: Use 'come down to' + a noun or -ing form: 'It comes down to trust' or 'It all comes down to whether we can afford it.' Don't confuse it with 'come down' meaning to fall or drop.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraSo are you taking the job in Leeds or staying in Bristol?
KofiI honestly can't decide. The pay is better in Leeds, but my friends are all here.
AmiraIs the new team any good?
KofiThey seem great. I think it comes down to whether I'm ready to start over somewhere new.
AmiraThen maybe visit for a weekend first and see how it feels.
KofiGood idea. I'll book a train tonight.

Show an example answer

I recently had to choose between two apartments. They were both nice, but it really came down to the commute. I didn't want to spend an extra hour on the train every day, so I picked the closer one.

Answer key: come down to

  1. 1. A. The main thing that will decide the choice is
  2. 2. comes down to
  3. 3. A. In the end, it came down to the course content.
  4. 4. Example: I recently had to choose between two apartments. They were both nice, but it really came down to the commute. I didn't want to spend an extra hour on the train every day, so I picked the closer one.
