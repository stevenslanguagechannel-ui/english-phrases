go, go, go

ExpressionLevel B1CasualTime & effort

Very busy, with no time to rest.

Work has been go, go, go all week, so I'm resting today.

Tip: Use it after 'be' or 'have been' to describe a stretch of time (a week, a month, a season) when you never stop; it's informal, so save it for conversation rather than formal writing.

Hear it in conversation

KenAre you free for dinner on Saturday? It feels like ages since we talked.
OlenaI'd love to, but I can't promise anything. The move has been go, go, go since the start of the month.
KenStill packing boxes?
OlenaPacking, cleaning, signing forms. Then the movers come on Friday.
KenOkay, so let's wait until the weekend after. Once you're settled in, I'll bring dinner to you.
OlenaThat's a deal. Thanks, Ken.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time in your life when you were extremely busy. What was happening?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few months ago I was studying for exams and working part-time, and it was go, go, go every single day. I got up early, went to classes, then worked until late and studied after that. It was tiring, but I learned how to plan my time much better.

Answer key: go, go, go

  1. 1. D. Very busy, with no time to rest
  2. 2. go, go, go
  3. 3. B. Honestly, it was go, go, go. I barely sat down until Friday evening.
  4. 4. Example: A few months ago I was studying for exams and working part-time, and it was go, go, go every single day. I got up early, went to classes, then worked until late and studied after that. It was tiring, but I learned how to plan my time much better.
