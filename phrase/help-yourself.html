Help yourself

ExpressionLevel B1CasualEveryday life

Take whatever you want. You don't need to ask.

The snacks are on the table. Help yourself!

Tip: Use it as a short invitation, often with 'to' plus a thing: 'Help yourself to some coffee.' Trap: it's for offering things, not for helping someone with a task.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaCome in, Kofi! Sorry about the mess. I've just finished baking.
KofiIt smells amazing in here. Are those lemon cookies?
OlenaThey are. Help yourself, there are plenty.
KofiAre you sure? They look like they took ages.
OlenaHonestly, I made far too many. Take a few home for later, too.
KofiThanks, Olena. Just one for now, then. I'll save the rest for the bus.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "Help yourself" mean?

2. Fill the gap

The fruit bowl is in the living room, so please whenever you're hungry.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: Is it okay if I borrow a pen from your desk?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had guests at your home. How did you make them feel welcome?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month my cousins came to stay for the weekend, and I wanted them to feel relaxed. I showed them where the tea and snacks were and said, "Help yourself to anything, whenever you like." By the second day they were making their own breakfast, which made me really happy.

Answer key: Help yourself

  1. 1. D. Take some cookies if you want them. You don't need to ask.
  2. 2. help yourself
  3. 3. B. Of course, help yourself. They're in the top drawer.
  4. 4. Example: Last month my cousins came to stay for the weekend, and I wanted them to feel relaxed. I showed them where the tea and snacks were and said, "Help yourself to anything, whenever you like." By the second day they were making their own breakfast, which made me really happy.
