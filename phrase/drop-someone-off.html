drop someone off

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To drop someone off is to take them somewhere by car and let them get out.

Can you drop me off at the airport on Friday?

Tip: Put the person between the words ('drop her off') or use 'drop off' with the place after it; with a pronoun you must say 'drop me off', never 'drop off me'.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraThe party starts at eight, but my car's in the garage until Thursday.
DiegoNo problem. I'm driving past your street anyway.
AmiraReally? Could you drop me off at Olena's place, then?
DiegoSure. Is it the flat with the green door?
AmiraYes, just stop at the corner and I'll walk the rest.
DiegoPerfect. I'll text you when I'm outside.

Answer key: drop someone off

  1. 1. D. Take me there by car and let me get out
  2. 2. drops off
  3. 3. B. Don't worry, I can drop you off on my way in.
