the walls have ears

IdiomLevel B2CasualOpinions & honesty

Someone nearby might be listening, so be careful what you say.

Let's talk about the surprise later. The walls have ears.

Tip: Say it as a short warning before or after a private comment, usually when other people could overhear; it is a fixed phrase, so keep 'walls' plural and don't change 'have'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you hear? They might close the Leeds office next year.
KofiWait, who told you that? I thought it was still confidential.
OlenaSomeone in HR mentioned it yesterday. I don't know how reliable it is.
KofiLet's go and get some air. The walls have ears in this building.
OlenaGood idea. I'll grab my coat.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you needed to keep something private but other people were close by.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month my friend wanted to tell me about a job she'd been offered, but we were sitting in a busy café. I whispered, 'Let's talk outside, the walls have ears.' We walked to the park and she told me everything there.

Answer key: the walls have ears

  1. 1. D. Someone nearby might overhear, so they should be careful what they say.
  2. 2. The walls have ears
  3. 3. A. Maybe wait. The walls have ears, and she might hear us.
  4. 4. Example: Last month my friend wanted to tell me about a job she'd been offered, but we were sitting in a busy café. I whispered, 'Let's talk outside, the walls have ears.' We walked to the park and she told me everything there.
