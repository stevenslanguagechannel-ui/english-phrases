get by

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

To manage with just enough skill, money or knowledge.

My Spanish isn't perfect, but it's enough to get by on holiday.

Tip: Use 'get by' on its own or with 'on' or 'with' (get by on a small salary, get by with basic English); it means you cope, not that you do well.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraHow's the new job in Lisbon? Is the language a problem?
KofiA bit. I only know a few words of Portuguese, but I get by in shops and cafés.
AmiraThat's a good start. What about at work?
KofiEveryone there speaks English, so that's easy.
AmiraGreat. Are you taking lessons?
KofiYes, an evening class starts next month. I want to do more than just point at things!

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I travelled to Japan with only a few phrases in Japanese. It was stressful at first, but I could order food and ask for directions, so I got by. People were patient, and I learned more every day.

Answer key: get by

  1. 1. C. To manage well enough in a situation, even if you're not an expert
  2. 2. get by
  3. 3. A. Yes, but I get by if I cook at home and don't spend much.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I travelled to Japan with only a few phrases in Japanese. It was stressful at first, but I could order food and ask for directions, so I got by. People were patient, and I learned more every day.
