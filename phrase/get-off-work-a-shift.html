get off (work / a shift)

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualWork & study

To finish work for the day.

I get off work at six, so let's meet at seven.

Tip: Use it as get off + work/a shift (or just 'get off' with a time); it's about when you leave, so it's not the same as 'get off a bus'.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraAre you free for dinner on Thursday?
KofiMaybe. I'm on the late shift that day.
AmiraOh no. What time do you get off?
KofiUsually around nine, but sometimes it's later.
AmiraThen let's do Friday instead. You'll be less tired.
KofiGood idea. Friday works.

4. Your turn: speak

Describe a typical weekday for you. What time do you finish, and what do you usually do afterwards?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I usually get off work around five thirty, though it can be later when we're busy. After that I go home, cook something simple, and call a friend or watch a film to relax.

Answer key: get off (work / a shift)

  1. 1. D. Finish work for the day
  2. 2. get off
  3. 3. A. I can't. I don't get off until seven tonight.
  4. 4. Example: I usually get off work around five thirty, though it can be later when we're busy. After that I go home, cook something simple, and call a friend or watch a film to relax.
