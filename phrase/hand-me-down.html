hand-me-down

ExpressionLevel B2CasualEveryday life

Something such as clothes that was used by someone else first and then given to you.

This jacket is a hand-me-down from my brother.

Tip: Use it as a noun (a hand-me-down, hand-me-downs) or an adjective before a noun (hand-me-down clothes); it's most common for clothes, toys and furniture passed within a family or between friends.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYour daughter's coat is lovely. Did you buy it recently?
KofiNo, it's a hand-me-down from my niece. She grew out of it after one winter.
OlenaIt looks brand new!
KofiI know. My daughter's wardrobe is mostly things from cousins, and honestly, it saves us a fortune.
OlenaI should ask my sister for her kids' old stuff. Mine grow so fast.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "hand-me-down" mean?

2. Fill the gap

Most of the furniture in his first flat was from his parents, so he hardly spent anything.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: Wow, those boots look almost new! Where did you get them?

4. Your turn: speak

Did you ever get clothes or other things from an older relative or friend? How did you feel about it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Yes, as a kid I wore my older brother's hand-me-downs all the time, especially jackets and sweaters. At first I was a bit annoyed because I wanted new things, but now I think it was sensible, and my parents saved a lot of money.

Answer key: hand-me-down

  1. 1. A. Something that was used by someone else first and then given to you
  2. 2. hand-me-downs
  3. 3. B. Thanks! They're hand-me-downs from my cousin, and they still fit perfectly.
  4. 4. Example: Yes, as a kid I wore my older brother's hand-me-downs all the time, especially jackets and sweaters. At first I was a bit annoyed because I wanted new things, but now I think it was sensible, and my parents saved a lot of money.
