run into someone

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralPeople & relationships

To run into someone is to meet them by chance.

I ran into my old teacher at the airport yesterday.

Tip: Say 'run into' + a person, and use it for unplanned meetings; if the object is a pronoun it goes in the middle ('I ran into her'), and don't use it for meetings you arranged.

Hear it in conversation

MeiYou look happy today. Good weekend?
DiegoReally good. I ran into Kofi at the market on Saturday.
MeiKofi from university? I haven't seen him in years!
DiegoSame here. We had coffee and talked for two hours.
MeiThat's so nice. Is he still living in Leeds?
DiegoNo, he moved back last month. I've got his number now, so we can all meet up soon.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "ran into Kofi" mean?

2. Fill the gap

I didn't expect to see anyone I knew on holiday, but I my neighbour at the beach.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: Guess who I saw at the supermarket this morning? Our old manager!

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you met someone unexpectedly. Where were you, and how did you feel?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last summer I was on a trip in another city, and I ran into a friend from my old job at a train station. We were both surprised, and we had a quick coffee together before my train left. It made the day feel special.

Answer key: run into someone

  1. 1. C. Met Kofi by chance, without planning to
  2. 2. ran into
  3. 3. A. Oh really? I ran into her last week too, outside the bank.
  4. 4. Example: Last summer I was on a trip in another city, and I ran into a friend from my old job at a train station. We were both surprised, and we had a quick coffee together before my train left. It made the day feel special.
