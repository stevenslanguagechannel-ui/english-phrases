under your breath

ExpressionLevel B2NeutralFeelings & reactions

In a very quiet voice, so other people cannot hear well.

He said something under his breath when the bus was late.

Tip: Use it after a verb of speaking, such as say, mutter, mumble or swear: 'She muttered something under her breath.' It usually suggests the person is annoyed or doesn't want to be heard, so don't use it for friendly whispering.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you see Kofi's face when the manager changed the deadline again?
KenI did. He smiled, but he was saying something under his breath the whole time.
OlenaCould you hear what it was?
KenNot really. I was standing too far away, but it didn't look polite.
OlenaWell, I don't blame him. That's the third change this week.

3. Choose the best reply

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you were annoyed but didn't want to show it. What did you do?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month my train was cancelled for the second time in a week, and I was already late for work. I didn't want to make a scene at the station, so I just said a few unhappy words under my breath and went to find another route.

Answer key: under your breath

  1. 1. A. In a very quiet voice, so others can't hear clearly
  2. 2. under her breath
  3. 3. B. No, I only muttered something under my breath. I didn't want to start an argument.
  4. 4. Example: Last month my train was cancelled for the second time in a week, and I was already late for work. I didn't want to make a scene at the station, so I just said a few unhappy words under my breath and went to find another route.
