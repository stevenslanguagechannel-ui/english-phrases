foot the bill

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To pay the cost of something for yourself or for other people.

My boss said the company will foot the bill for the training course.

Tip: Use 'foot the bill (for something)' when someone pays a cost, often one that others might expect to share; it is not used for tiny amounts, and 'foot' stays the same in every tense (foots, footed, footing).

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you book the flights for the conference yet?
KofiNot yet. I'm waiting to hear who's paying for them.
OlenaI thought the department would cover it.
KofiMaybe, but last year we all had to foot the bill ourselves and claim it back months later.
OlenaUgh, I remember. I'll email finance today and ask.
