catch up on

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralWork & study

To do something you didn't have time to do earlier.

I'm going to catch up on my emails after lunch.

Tip: Use 'catch up on' + the thing you need to do (catch up on sleep, work, reading); don't confuse it with 'catch up with' a person, which means to meet and talk after a long time.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look tired, Kofi. Was the trip okay?
KofiIt was great, but I got back at midnight and I haven't opened my laptop in a week.
OlenaSo what's the plan for today?
KofiI'm going to stay at my desk and catch up on the reports I missed.
OlenaGood luck. I'll bring you a coffee later.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to catch up after being away or very busy. What did you do?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I was ill for almost a week, so I missed a lot of lessons at university. When I felt better, I spent the whole weekend trying to catch up on my notes and homework. It was tiring, but I felt much calmer on Monday.

Answer key: catch up on

  1. 1. D. To do work that you didn't have time to do before
  2. 2. catch up on
  3. 3. C. I'm just trying to catch up on a few things I didn't finish this week.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I was ill for almost a week, so I missed a lot of lessons at university. When I felt better, I spent the whole weekend trying to catch up on my notes and homework. It was tiring, but I felt much calmer on Monday.
