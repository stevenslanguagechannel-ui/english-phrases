get the ball rolling

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

To start something so other people can join in.

I'll get the ball rolling and send the invitations tonight.

Tip: Say 'get the ball rolling' when you take the first step so others can follow; it's often used with 'I'll', 'let's' or 'to help'.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraOur study group still hasn't picked a time to meet. Everyone's waiting for someone else to suggest something.
KofiTypical. Nobody wants to go first.
AmiraRight. I'll get the ball rolling and post a few options in the chat.
KofiGood idea. I can do Tuesday or Thursday evening.
AmiraPerfect, that's two votes already. Let's see who else replies.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you were the first person to start something in a group, such as a project, a plan or a conversation.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year my friends wanted to organise a surprise party, but nobody was doing anything. I decided to get the ball rolling by creating a group chat and suggesting a date. After that, everyone started sharing ideas, and it came together quickly.

Answer key: get the ball rolling

  1. 1. A. Make the first move so the group can start joining in
  2. 2. get the ball rolling
  3. 3. B. Why don't I get the ball rolling by making a shared document?
  4. 4. Example: Last year my friends wanted to organise a surprise party, but nobody was doing anything. I decided to get the ball rolling by creating a group chat and suggesting a date. After that, everyone started sharing ideas, and it came together quickly.
