hit a plateau

IdiomLevel B2NeutralSuccess & progress

When you hit a plateau, you stop making progress for a while.

My running speed hit a plateau, so I changed my training.

Tip: Use it as 'hit a plateau' (or 'reach a plateau') about skills, results, or numbers; it is often followed by 'so' plus a change of plan, and the past tense is most common.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaHow's the Spanish going? You were doing so well a few months ago.
DiegoHonestly, I think I've hit a plateau. I know the same words every week.
OlenaAre you still doing the same app lessons every day?
DiegoYes, the same ones. I just repeat them.
OlenaMaybe try talking to real people. That might get you moving again.
DiegoGood idea. There's a language café near my office on Thursdays.

Show an example answer

I started learning the guitar last year, and for a few months I improved quickly. Then I hit a plateau, and I kept playing the same easy songs. I fixed it by learning a harder piece each week, and now I'm moving forward again.
