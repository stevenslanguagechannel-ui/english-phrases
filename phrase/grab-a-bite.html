grab a bite

ExpressionLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To have a quick, simple meal or snack.

Let's grab a bite after work.

Tip: Use it as a verb phrase: 'grab a bite' or 'grab a bite to eat', often with 'let's' or 'want to'; it suggests something quick and informal, not a long restaurant meal.

Hear it in conversation

KenAre you free for a bit? I've been in back-to-back meetings since nine.
OlenaI've got about forty minutes before my next call.
KenPerfect. Want to grab a bite in the café downstairs?
OlenaYes, please. I skipped breakfast and I'm starving.
KenSame here. I'll get us a table.
OlenaGreat. I'll be there in five minutes.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you were very busy but still had to eat. What did you do?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month I was moving flats, so I had no time for a proper lunch. I just grabbed a bite at a bakery near my old building and went straight back to carrying boxes. It wasn't much, but it gave me enough energy to finish the day.

Answer key: grab a bite

  1. 1. D. Have something small to eat without making a big occasion of it
  2. 2. grab a bite
  3. 3. A. Sure, let's grab a bite nearby and then head back.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I was moving flats, so I had no time for a proper lunch. I just grabbed a bite at a bakery near my old building and went straight back to carrying boxes. It wasn't much, but it gave me enough energy to finish the day.
