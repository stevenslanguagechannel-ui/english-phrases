hang out

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualPeople & relationships

To spend free time with friends in a relaxed way.

We usually hang out at the park on Saturdays.

Tip: Say 'hang out with someone' or 'hang out at/in a place'; it's an intransitive phrasal verb, so don't add an object directly (not 'hang out my friends').

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre you free after your exam on Friday?
KofiYes! I'm done by noon. Why, what's the plan?
OlenaNothing big. Some of us are going to hang out by the river and get food.
KofiThat sounds perfect. I need to switch my brain off for a few hours.
OlenaGreat. Text me when you leave the exam room and we'll find you.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a place where you like to spend time with friends. What do you do there?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

There's a small café near my university where my friends and I like to hang out. We don't do much, just chat, share snacks and sometimes study a bit. It's a good way to relax after a busy week.

Answer key: hang out

  1. 1. D. Spend relaxed time together without a strict plan
  2. 2. hang out
  3. 3. B. I usually hang out with my neighbours and have tea in the garden.
  4. 4. Example: There's a small café near my university where my friends and I like to hang out. We don't do much, just chat, share snacks and sometimes study a bit. It's a good way to relax after a busy week.
