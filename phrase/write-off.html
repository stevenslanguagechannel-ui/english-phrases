write off

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralPeople & relationships

To decide that someone or something is not worth your time or has no chance.

Don't write him off yet. He might still win.

Tip: Use it with a person or thing as the object: 'write someone off' or 'write it off'. A pronoun goes in the middle, so say 'write him off', not 'write off him'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you see Kofi's first draft for the client pitch? It's pretty rough.
KenI did. Honestly, I was ready to write it off.
OlenaReally? He's only had two days to work on it.
KenTrue. And his last project started badly too, then it turned out great.
OlenaExactly. Let's give him until Friday and then look again.
KenFine. I'll send him some notes this afternoon.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when people thought you or someone you know would fail, but things turned out well.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I started my first job, a few colleagues wrote me off because I was quiet and made some early mistakes. I didn't argue with them. I just kept learning, and after about six months I got the best feedback in my team. It taught me not to write other people off too quickly.

Answer key: write off

  1. 1. A. Decide something is not worth more effort or has no chance
  2. 2. write off
  3. 3. B. Don't write yourself off. Plenty of people need three or four tries.
  4. 4. Example: When I started my first job, a few colleagues wrote me off because I was quiet and made some early mistakes. I didn't argue with them. I just kept learning, and after about six months I got the best feedback in my team. It taught me not to write other people off too quickly.
