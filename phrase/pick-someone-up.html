pick someone up

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To go to a place in a car and collect someone.

Can you pick me up from the airport at nine?

Tip: Put the person between the verb and 'up' ('pick her up'), but with a pronoun you must split it: say 'pick me up', never 'pick up me'.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraMy flight lands at six tomorrow, and the train strike starts at midnight.
KofiThat's annoying. How are you getting home?
AmiraNo idea yet. I'd take a taxi, but it's so expensive at that time.
KofiDon't bother. I'll pick you up. I'm awake early anyway.
AmiraReally? You're a lifesaver. I'll text you when I land.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time someone helped you with transport. What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last winter my car wouldn't start before an important meeting, and I was panicking. My neighbour offered to pick me up and drive me to the office, so I got there with five minutes to spare. I bought her lunch the next week to say thank you.

Answer key: pick someone up

  1. 1. A. Drive to the airport and take you home in a car
  2. 2. pick me up
  3. 3. A. Stay there. I'll pick you up in ten minutes.
  4. 4. Example: Last winter my car wouldn't start before an important meeting, and I was panicking. My neighbour offered to pick me up and drive me to the office, so I got there with five minutes to spare. I bought her lunch the next week to say thank you.
