get rid of

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

To throw away or remove something you do not want or need.

I need to get rid of these old boxes before we move.

Tip: Use it with an object: get rid of + something (get rid of the sofa); the object can go between nothing, and you can't say 'get rid' alone. Past form: got rid of.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraHave you finished packing for the move?
KenNot even close. My flat is full of stuff I never use.
AmiraSame thing happened to me last year. Are you keeping the big bookshelf?
KenNo, it's too heavy. I'm going to get rid of it this weekend.
AmiraMy cousin is looking for one. I can ask if she wants it.
KenThat would be great. It's better than paying someone to take it away.

Show an example answer

Last month I got rid of my old laptop. It was so slow that it made working from home really frustrating. I gave it to a local repair shop, and now I have a lot more space on my desk.

Answer key: get rid of

  1. 1. D. To stop using something because you do not want or need it any more
  2. 2. get rid of
  3. 3. C. I know. I really should get rid of all these old papers.
  4. 4. Example: Last month I got rid of my old laptop. It was so slow that it made working from home really frustrating. I gave it to a local repair shop, and now I have a lot more space on my desk.
