part and parcel

IdiomLevel B2NeutralWork & study

Something that is a normal, necessary part of a situation or job.

Early mornings are part and parcel of being a baker.

Tip: Use 'X is part and parcel of Y' (or 'come as part and parcel of Y'); it often follows a downside, and the phrase is fixed, so don't change the word order or say 'part and a parcel'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look exhausted, Kofi. Another late shift at the clinic?
KofiYes, and I had three patients who were really upset today. I'm starting to wonder if I'm cut out for this.
OlenaHonestly, I think difficult days are part and parcel of nursing. Even the most experienced nurses get them.
KofiI suppose you're right. It's just harder than I expected in my first year.
OlenaIt gets easier. Go home, eat something proper and get some sleep.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a job or activity that has some unpleasant parts. How do you feel about them?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I've been learning the piano for two years, and practising scales is part and parcel of it. It can be really boring, but I know I can't get better without it. So I try to treat it as a warm-up and not as a chore.

Answer key: part and parcel

  1. 1. D. A normal and unavoidable part of something
  2. 2. part and parcel
  3. 3. A. Well, it's part and parcel of working in a hospital, I'm afraid.
  4. 4. Example: I've been learning the piano for two years, and practising scales is part and parcel of it. It can be really boring, but I know I can't get better without it. So I try to treat it as a warm-up and not as a chore.
