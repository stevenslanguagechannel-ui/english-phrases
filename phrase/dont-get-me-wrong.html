Don't get me wrong

ExpressionLevel B1NeutralOpinions & honesty

A phrase you say before a 'but' to stop someone from misunderstanding you.

Don't get me wrong, I love my job, but I'm very tired this week.

Tip: Say it before you give a criticism or a different opinion, then follow it with 'but'; it softens what comes next, so don't use it for good news.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaSo, what did you think of the new flat? You've been quiet since we left.
KofiDon't get me wrong, the kitchen is lovely, but the bus stop is twenty minutes away.
OlenaTrue, that's a long walk, especially in winter.
KofiAnd the rent is already at the top of what I can pay.
OlenaThen maybe keep looking. There's no rush, right?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a place you have lived in or visited that you liked, but that wasn't perfect.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I spent a month in Lisbon last year. Don't get me wrong, I loved the food and the people, but the hills made walking around really tiring. I would still go back, maybe with better shoes.

Answer key: Don't get me wrong

  1. 1. D. I want you to understand that I like the kitchen, even though I have a problem with the flat.
  2. 2. Don't get me wrong
  3. 3. B. Don't get me wrong, it's useful, but it takes me a long time to learn all the buttons.
  4. 4. Example: I spent a month in Lisbon last year. Don't get me wrong, I loved the food and the people, but the hills made walking around really tiring. I would still go back, maybe with better shoes.
