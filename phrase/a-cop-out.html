a cop-out

ExpressionLevel B2CasualOpinions & honesty

An excuse or choice that avoids something difficult or that you should do.

Saying you forgot is a cop-out. You just didn't want to call.

Tip: Use it after 'is a' or 'was a' to criticise an excuse or an easy choice; it sounds judgmental, so be careful when you say it to someone directly.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you tell Kofi we're not coming to his party?
DiegoNot yet. I'm going to text him that I have a headache.
OlenaHonestly, that's a bit of a cop-out.
DiegoI know, but I hate saying no to him.
OlenaHe'd prefer the truth. Just tell him we're tired and need a quiet weekend.
DiegoOkay, you're right. I'll call him tonight.

3. Choose the best reply

Ken: I told my manager I couldn't finish the report because the printer was broken, but I could have sent it online.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you gave an excuse instead of the real reason. Why did you do it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I told my friends I was too busy to join a hiking trip, but really I was nervous about keeping up with them. Looking back, it was a total cop-out. I should have just been honest, and they would have walked more slowly for me.

Answer key: a cop-out

  1. 1. C. An easy excuse that avoids saying the honest thing
  2. 2. cop-out
  3. 3. A. Yeah, that sounds like a bit of a cop-out, to be honest.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I told my friends I was too busy to join a hiking trip, but really I was nervous about keeping up with them. Looking back, it was a total cop-out. I should have just been honest, and they would have walked more slowly for me.
