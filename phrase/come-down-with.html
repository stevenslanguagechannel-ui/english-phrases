come down with

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralProblems & solutions

To start to get an illness, like a cold or the flu.

I think I'm coming down with a cold.

Tip: Use 'come down with' + an illness (a cold, the flu, a stomach bug); it is about the illness starting, so you often say it before you feel fully ill.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou're not coming to the study group tonight?
KofiI don't think so. My throat has been sore since lunch, and I keep sneezing.
OlenaOh no. Do you think you're coming down with something?
KofiProbably. Half my office is off sick this week.
OlenaGo home and rest. I'll send you my notes later.
KofiThanks, Olena. That's really kind.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you felt ill at a bad moment, for example before a trip or an important day.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I was coming down with a cold the night before a work trip. I felt tired and my nose was blocked, but I still went. I wish I had stayed home to rest, because by the next day I felt much worse.

Answer key: come down with

  1. 1. B. Starting to get an illness
  2. 2. coming down with
  3. 3. A. I think I'm coming down with a cold, so I'll stay away from you.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I was coming down with a cold the night before a work trip. I felt tired and my nose was blocked, but I still went. I wish I had stayed home to rest, because by the next day I felt much worse.
