pinch pennies

IdiomLevel B2CasualEveryday life

To be very careful with money and spend as little as possible.

We're pinching pennies this month because of the new car.

Tip: Use it as a verb phrase in any tense: 'We've been pinching pennies', 'I'm trying to pinch pennies'. It describes a period of careful budgeting, not one purchase, and it isn't used for being mean with other people.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraAre you coming to the beach house weekend in March?
KofiI'd love to, but I'm still pinching pennies until the roof is fixed.
AmiraOh no, was it expensive?
KofiWay more than I expected. I'm bringing my own lunch every day now.
AmiraWell, we'll split the cost of the house six ways, so it won't be much. Think about it.
KofiOkay, send me the numbers and I'll see.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you had to be careful with your money. What did you do?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I first moved abroad for my studies, I was really pinching pennies for about a year. I cooked all my meals, walked instead of taking the bus and only bought second-hand books. It wasn't always fun, but it taught me how to budget properly.

Answer key: pinch pennies

  1. 1. D. Being very careful about spending money
  2. 2. pinching pennies
  3. 3. C. Maybe next time, I'm pinching pennies at the moment. Could we cook at mine instead?
  4. 4. Example: When I first moved abroad for my studies, I was really pinching pennies for about a year. I cooked all my meals, walked instead of taking the bus and only bought second-hand books. It wasn't always fun, but it taught me how to budget properly.
