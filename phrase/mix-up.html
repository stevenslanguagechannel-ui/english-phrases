mix-up

ExpressionLevel B2NeutralProblems & solutions

A mistake that happens when things get confused.

Sorry, there's been a mix-up with your booking.

Tip: Use it as a noun: 'a mix-up with/over something' or 'there's been a mix-up'; the verb is 'mix up' (two words), as in 'I mixed up the dates'.

Hear it in conversation

KenAmira, did you get my email about the meeting room?
AmiraI did, but I'm confused. I booked Room 4 for ten o'clock.
KenThat's strange. The system shows Room 4 as mine, not yours.
AmiraOh, I think there was a mix-up with the names. We both have a Room 4 booking.
KenLet's ask reception to sort it out. We can share the room if we have to.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when something went wrong because of a misunderstanding or confusion.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I went to the airport on the wrong day because of a mix-up with my ticket dates. I had read the email too quickly and confused two flights. Luckily the airline moved me to the next flight, so it only cost me a few hours.

Answer key: mix-up

  1. 1. A. A mistake caused by things being confused with each other
  2. 2. mix-up
  3. 3. A. Sorry, there was a mix-up with the addresses. I'll get it back for you.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I went to the airport on the wrong day because of a mix-up with my ticket dates. I had read the email too quickly and confused two flights. Luckily the airline moved me to the next flight, so it only cost me a few hours.
