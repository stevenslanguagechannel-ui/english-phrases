dial back

Phrasal verbLevel B2NeutralEveryday life

To reduce something, like how much you do or spend.

I'm going to dial back my coffee habit this month.

Tip: Use it with an object: dial back + noun (dial back your spending, dial back the hours); it often follows 'need to' or 'going to', and the opposite is 'step up' or 'ramp up'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaYou look exhausted, Kofi. Are you still doing that early gym session every day?
KofiYes, six days a week. My knees really hurt now, though.
OlenaMaybe you should dial back a bit and give your body a rest.
KofiYou're probably right. I'll cut it to three mornings and see how I feel.
OlenaGood plan. You'll probably enjoy it more, too.

4. Your turn: speak

Is there something in your daily life that you would like to do less of? Tell me about it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I'd like to dial back the time I spend on my phone, especially in the evening. I usually scroll for hours without noticing, so I'm trying to set a limit and read a book instead.

Answer key: dial back

  1. 1. C. Do less of something than before
  2. 2. dial back
  3. 3. A. You should dial back your hours before you burn out.
  4. 4. Example: I'd like to dial back the time I spend on my phone, especially in the evening. I usually scroll for hours without noticing, so I'm trying to set a limit and read a book instead.
