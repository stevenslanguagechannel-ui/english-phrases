pull a muscle

ExpressionLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

To hurt a muscle by stretching or using it too hard.

I pulled a muscle in my leg at the gym.

Tip: Use it as a verb phrase in any tense ("I pulled a muscle", "He's pulled a muscle"), and name the body part with "in my back/calf/shoulder"; it describes a sudden injury, not general tiredness.

Hear it in conversation

KofiAre you still coming to the five-a-side game tonight, Ken?
KenI'd love to, but I pulled a muscle in my calf on Tuesday.
KofiOuch. Is it bad?
KenIt's better than it was, but I can't run yet. The doctor said to rest it for a week.
KofiThen stay home. Come and watch us next time instead.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "pulled a muscle" mean?

2. Fill the gap

Amira can't carry the shopping because she in her shoulder while painting the ceiling.

3. Choose the best reply

Olena: Why did you stop halfway through your run this morning?

4. Your turn: speak

Have you ever had a small injury that stopped you from doing something you enjoy? What happened?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Yes, last winter I pulled a muscle in my back while moving a sofa with my brother. I couldn't go to my yoga class for two weeks, which was really frustrating. Now I always warm up properly before I lift anything heavy.

Answer key: pull a muscle

  1. 1. D. Hurt a muscle by using or stretching it too much
  2. 2. pulled a muscle
  3. 3. B. I pulled a muscle in my thigh, so I walked home.
  4. 4. Example: Yes, last winter I pulled a muscle in my back while moving a sofa with my brother. I couldn't go to my yoga class for two weeks, which was really frustrating. Now I always warm up properly before I lift anything heavy.
