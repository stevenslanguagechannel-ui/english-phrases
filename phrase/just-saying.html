just saying

ExpressionLevel B2CasualOpinions & honesty

A light phrase you add after a comment to say you don't want to argue or cause trouble.

That jacket looks a bit small. Just saying!

Tip: Add it at the end of a comment, often with a falling tone, to soften an opinion or a warning; it's casual, so avoid it in formal writing or serious complaints.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre you really going to leave for the airport at six? Your flight isn't until nine.
KofiYeah, I hate rushing. I'd rather wait at the gate.
OlenaFair enough. But the motorway is always packed on Friday mornings.
KofiHmm, that's true. Last time it took me almost two hours.
OlenaExactly. I'm just saying. Leave when you like, of course.
KofiNo, I'll go at five thirty. Thanks for the warning.

2. Fill the gap

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: You're going to wear that thin coat to the mountains? It's supposed to snow.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you gave a friend or colleague some advice they didn't ask for. How did you say it?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last month my colleague was about to send a long email to a client without checking the numbers. I said, "Maybe look at the figures again. Just saying!" She laughed, checked, and found a small mistake, so I was glad I spoke up.

Answer key: just saying

  1. 1. C. Olena is giving a gentle warning and doesn't want to start an argument.
  2. 2. Just saying
  3. 3. C. Just saying, but you might want to bring something warmer.
  4. 4. Example: Last month my colleague was about to send a long email to a client without checking the numbers. I said, "Maybe look at the figures again. Just saying!" She laughed, checked, and found a small mistake, so I was glad I spoke up.
