sweep it under the rug

IdiomLevel B2NeutralProblems & solutions

To hide a problem instead of dealing with it.

Don't sweep it under the rug. Let's fix the problem now.

Tip: Use 'sweep something under the rug' (or 'carpet' in British English) for a problem someone hides to avoid dealing with it, often in the pattern 'try to sweep it under the rug' or 'can't just sweep it under the rug'.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you see the budget numbers for the festival? We're about two thousand over.
KofiI did. Our manager wants us to sweep it under the rug and hope nobody checks.
OlenaThat's risky. The finance team will notice sooner or later.
KofiI know. I'm going to ask for a meeting tomorrow and explain what happened.
OlenaGood idea. It's easier to fix if we're honest about it now.

4. Your turn: speak

Describe a time when someone avoided dealing with a problem. What happened, and what do you think they should have done?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

A few years ago, our neighbours noticed a small leak in the shared roof, but everyone tried to sweep it under the rug because nobody wanted to pay for it. Months later the damage was much worse and the repair cost three times as much. I think they should have spoken up straight away and shared the cost while it was still small.

Answer key: sweep it under the rug

  1. 1. D. Hide the problem and avoid dealing with it
  2. 2. sweep it under the rug
  3. 3. C. Don't sweep it under the rug. Tell them early and explain how you'll fix it.
  4. 4. Example: A few years ago, our neighbours noticed a small leak in the shared roof, but everyone tried to sweep it under the rug because nobody wanted to pay for it. Months later the damage was much worse and the repair cost three times as much. I think they should have spoken up straight away and shared the cost while it was still small.
