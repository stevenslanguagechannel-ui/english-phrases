off the grid

IdiomLevel B2CasualEveryday life

To go off the grid is to live or spend time without phones, internet or other modern technology.

I'm going off the grid this weekend. No phone, no email.

Tip: Use it with 'go' or 'be' (go off the grid, be off the grid), and remember it means choosing to disconnect, not just having no signal.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you get my messages last week? I tried calling you twice.
KofiSorry! I was staying at my uncle's cabin in the hills. I went off the grid for five days.
OlenaFive days? Didn't you get bored?
KofiNot at all. I read, cooked and walked a lot. I feel like a new person.
OlenaOkay, I'm jealous. My phone is attached to my hand.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does 'went off the grid' mean?

2. Fill the gap

After a stressful month, Amira decided to for a few days and leave her laptop at home.

3. Choose the best reply

Diego: I'm so tired of notifications. I check my phone about a hundred times a day.

4. Your turn: speak

Do you ever try to spend time away from your phone or the internet? Tell me about it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Yes, a few times a year I try to go off the grid for a weekend, usually by going camping with friends. I leave my phone in a bag and only use it in an emergency. It's hard on the first day, but by the second day I feel much calmer.

Answer key: off the grid

  1. 1. B. Stayed away from phones, internet and modern technology for a while
  2. 2. go off the grid
  3. 3. B. Maybe you should go off the grid for a weekend and see how it feels.
  4. 4. Example: Yes, a few times a year I try to go off the grid for a weekend, usually by going camping with friends. I leave my phone in a bag and only use it in an emergency. It's hard on the first day, but by the second day I feel much calmer.
