warm up

Phrasal verbLevel B1NeutralEveryday life

To do light exercise before sport so your body is ready.

Always warm up before you go for a run.

Tip: Use 'warm up' (no object) or 'warm up for something'; the noun is 'warm-up' and 'warm up' can also mean to heat food or make an engine hotter, so check the context.

Hear it in conversation

KenAre you ready to start? I've got the cones set out on the pitch.
OlenaGive us five minutes. Half the team still needs to warm up.
KenFair enough. I don't want anyone pulling a muscle in the first game.
OlenaExactly. Some of them only just got out of the car.
KenOkay, I'll go and get the water bottles while we wait.

4. Your turn: speak

Do you do any exercise or sport? Tell me about how you get ready for it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I play badminton with friends on Saturday mornings. We always warm up for about ten minutes with some stretching and light jogging, because I once hurt my shoulder when I skipped it. Now I never start a game without doing it.

Answer key: warm up

  1. 1. C. Do gentle exercise before a game so your body is ready
  2. 2. warm up
  3. 3. B. Take it easy at first and warm up with a slow jog and some stretches.
  4. 4. Example: I play badminton with friends on Saturday mornings. We always warm up for about ten minutes with some stretching and light jogging, because I once hurt my shoulder when I skipped it. Now I never start a game without doing it.
