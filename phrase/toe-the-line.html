toe the line

IdiomLevel C1NeutralWork & study

To obey the rules and do what is expected.

New staff must toe the line for the first month while they learn the rules.

Tip: Often used with 'have to', 'make someone', or 'refuse to'; it can sound a little negative, as if the rules are strict or the person has no real choice.

Hear it in conversation

MeiDid Ravi get the manager job in the end?
DiegoHe did, and he's already a different person in meetings.
MeiReally? He used to argue with everyone.
DiegoI know. Now he has to toe the line, because the director is watching him closely.
MeiPoor guy. That sounds stressful.
DiegoHonestly, I think he'll be fine once he settles in.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I started my first job in a hospital office, I had to toe the line for the first few months because everything had to be done exactly the right way. It felt a bit restrictive at the time, but I learned a lot from it. Looking back, I think it helped me build good habits.
