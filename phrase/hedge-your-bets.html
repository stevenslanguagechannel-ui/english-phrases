hedge your bets

IdiomLevel B2NeutralProblems & solutions

To hedge your bets means to protect yourself by choosing more than one option, in case one goes wrong.

I booked two restaurants for Saturday, just to hedge my bets.

Tip: Use it as 'hedge your bets' (often 'to hedge my/our bets'), usually when you choose more than one option or avoid committing fully; it is not about gardens or fences, and it is more common in speech about plans, money and choices.

Hear it in conversation

MeiHave you decided which university to go for in the autumn?
DiegoNot yet. I've applied to three, and I'm also looking at a job in case I don't get in.
MeiThat's a lot of paperwork.
DiegoI know, but I'd rather hedge my bets than end up with nothing in September.
MeiFair enough. Which one is your favourite?
DiegoThe one near the coast, but I'm keeping my options open.

2. Fill the gap

I don't know which phone plan will suit us, so we're going to and try two for a month.

3. Choose the best reply

Ken: Are you really going to book both trains, just in case one is cancelled?

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time you had a few options and you didn't want to rely on just one. What did you do?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

When I was looking for a flat last year, I didn't want to wait for one landlord to reply, so I decided to hedge my bets. I arranged viewings with four different agents and kept two offers open at the same time. In the end it worked well, because the first flat fell through and I had a backup ready.

Answer key: hedge your bets

  1. 1. D. Protect myself by having more than one possible plan
  2. 2. hedge our bets
  3. 3. A. Yes, I'd rather hedge my bets than miss the meeting.
