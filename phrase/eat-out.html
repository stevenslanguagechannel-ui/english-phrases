eat out

Phrasal verbLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To have a meal in a restaurant instead of at home.

I'm too tired to cook, so let's eat out tonight.

Tip: Use 'eat out' without an object ('We eat out a lot'); if you name the place, add 'at': 'We ate out at a new Thai place'.

Hear it in conversation

MeiAre you free on Friday? A few of us are meeting after work.
DiegoYes! Are you cooking at your place?
MeiNo, my kitchen is a mess right now. We're going to eat out instead.
DiegoPerfect. Is there anywhere you'd recommend nearby?
MeiThere's a small Greek place on Park Street. I'll book a table for six.
DiegoGreat, I'll see you there at seven.

4. Your turn: speak

How often do you eat out, and what do you usually like to order?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I eat out about once a week, usually with friends on a Saturday. I love trying new places, and I normally order something I can't easily make at home, like a spicy curry or fresh pasta.

Answer key: eat out

  1. 1. C. Have a meal at a restaurant instead of at home
  2. 2. eat out
  3. 3. B. Let's eat out. I don't feel like washing up afterwards.
  4. 4. Example: I eat out about once a week, usually with friends on a Saturday. I love trying new places, and I normally order something I can't easily make at home, like a spicy curry or fresh pasta.
