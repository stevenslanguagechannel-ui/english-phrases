a leg up

IdiomLevel B2CasualWork & study

An advantage that helps you do better than others.

Her internship gave her a leg up when she applied for jobs.

Tip: Use it as 'give someone a leg up' or 'get a leg up'; it's friendly and common in everyday speech, but don't confuse it with 'pull someone's leg', which means to joke.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you hear? Kofi got the job at the design studio.
DiegoReally? That's great. He only finished his degree in June.
OlenaYes, but his uncle runs a similar company, and Kofi worked there every summer.
DiegoSo that gave him a leg up over the other candidates.
OlenaDefinitely. But he's also talented, so he deserves it.

4. Your turn: speak

Can you think of something that gave you a leg up in your studies or career? Tell me about it.

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

I think learning to type quickly really gave me a leg up when I started my first office job. I could finish reports faster than my colleagues, so my manager noticed me early on. It was a small skill, but it made a big difference.

Answer key: a leg up

  1. 1. D. An advantage that helps someone do better than others
  2. 2. a leg up
  3. 3. A. Don't worry. The summer course you took will give you a leg up.
  4. 4. Example: I think learning to type quickly really gave me a leg up when I started my first office job. I could finish reports faster than my colleagues, so my manager noticed me early on. It was a small skill, but it made a big difference.
