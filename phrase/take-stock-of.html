take stock of

IdiomLevel B2NeutralSuccess & progress

To stop and think carefully about what you have or where you are now.

At the end of the year, I like to take stock of what I have achieved.

Tip: Use it with an object: 'take stock of the situation / what we have / where things stand'. It sounds natural in work talk, but it is also fine with friends when life is changing.

Hear it in conversation

AmiraYou look tired, Kofi. Has the move been that bad?
KofiIt's chaos. Boxes everywhere, and I can't find anything.
AmiraBefore you unpack another thing, why not take stock of what you've actually got?
KofiGood idea. I probably own three kettles and no plates.
AmiraHa! Make a list, then keep only what you need.
KofiOkay. I'll do it this evening, room by room.

4. Your turn: speak

Describe a time when you stopped to think about your life or your goals. What did you decide?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last January I took stock of how I was spending my time, and I realised I was always too busy to exercise. So I cut a few things from my schedule and joined a swimming class. It was a small change, but I feel much healthier now.

Answer key: take stock of

  1. 1. A. Check carefully what you have and what the situation is
  2. 2. take stock of
  3. 3. C. Maybe it's time to take stock of what you really want from work.
  4. 4. Example: Last January I took stock of how I was spending my time, and I realised I was always too busy to exercise. So I cut a few things from my schedule and joined a swimming class. It was a small change, but I feel much healthier now.
