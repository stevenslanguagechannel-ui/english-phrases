give someone a ring

ExpressionLevel B1CasualEveryday life

To phone someone.

I'll give you a ring when I get to the airport.

Tip: Use 'give someone a ring' with a person as the object ('give me a ring', 'give Mum a ring'); it's mostly British, but Americans will understand it, and you shouldn't use it in very formal writing.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaAre we still meeting at the café on Saturday?
KofiI think so, but I need to check my shift first.
OlenaNo problem. When will you know?
KofiMy manager sends the rota tomorrow morning. I'll give you a ring once I've seen it.
OlenaPerfect. If I don't pick up, leave a message and I'll call you back.

Workbook

1. What does it mean?

In this conversation, what does "give you a ring" mean?

2. Fill the gap

My sister's flight lands at six, so I'll as soon as I'm in the car.

3. Choose the best reply

Amira: I've got the results of my interview, but I can't talk now. I'm on a train.

4. Your turn: speak

Tell me about a time when you needed to contact someone quickly. What did you do?

Answer out loud in 2–3 sentences and use the phrase. Then compare with the example answer.

Show an example answer

Last year I missed my bus and was going to be late for a meeting with a client. I gave my colleague a ring from the station and asked her to start without me. She was very understanding, and I arrived about twenty minutes later.

Answer key: give someone a ring

  1. 1. A. Phone you
  2. 2. give her a ring
  3. 3. B. No worries, I'll give you a ring this evening.
  4. 4. Example: Last year I missed my bus and was going to be late for a meeting with a client. I gave my colleague a ring from the station and asked her to start without me. She was very understanding, and I arrived about twenty minutes later.
