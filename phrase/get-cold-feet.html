get cold feet

IdiomLevel B1CasualFeelings & reactions

To suddenly feel too nervous to do something you planned.

Ken got cold feet just before his big presentation.

Tip: Use 'get cold feet' (or 'got cold feet') about a planned commitment or big step, such as a wedding, a move or a job change; it is not about being physically cold.

Hear it in conversation

OlenaDid you ever book that flight to Lisbon for your solo trip?
DiegoNot yet. I was so excited last month, but now I keep getting cold feet.
OlenaReally? What's worrying you?
DiegoI've never travelled alone before. What if I get lost or feel lonely?
OlenaHonestly, you'll meet people the first day. Book it tonight before you change your mind.
DiegoOkay, okay. I'll do it after dinner.

Show an example answer

A few years ago I planned to give a short talk at a local club, but the night before I got cold feet and almost cancelled. In the end I went anyway, because I didn't want to let the organisers down. It was stressful at first, but I felt really proud afterwards.

  2. 2. got cold feet
  3. 3. B. Don't get cold feet now. You've prepared for this for months.
