People & relationships

A conversational idiom workbook: 37 phrases, each with a short conversation and four quick tasks. Do it online and check your answers, or print it and use the answer key at the end.

In this workbook

  1. bail on: To cancel a plan at the last minute, especially when others are counting on you.
  2. bang on about: To talk about the same subject for too long, in a boring or annoying way.
  3. bookworm: A person who loves reading and reads a lot.
  4. break the ice: To make people feel relaxed when they first meet.
  5. a breath of fresh air: Someone or something new and different that makes things feel better.
  6. butter someone up: To say nice things to someone so they will do what you want.
  7. chip in: To give a small amount of money or help together with others.
  8. claim to fame: The one interesting or impressive thing a person or place is known for.
  9. count me in: A friendly way to say you want to join an activity.
  10. drop by: To visit someone for a short, informal time.
  11. get along with: To have a friendly relationship with someone.
  12. hang out: To spend free time with friends in a relaxed way.
  13. hit it off: To like each other immediately when you meet.
  14. keep in touch: To stay in contact with someone by calling or messaging.
  15. keep up appearances: To act as if everything is fine or normal, even when it isn't.
  16. lend a hand: To help someone with a job.
  17. the life of the party: A person who is full of fun and makes a party exciting.
  18. make it up to you: To do something nice to fix a problem you caused.
  19. make yourself at home: Relax and feel comfortable in someone's house.
  20. meet halfway: To reach a compromise where each side gives a little.
  21. mind your own business: Stop asking about things that are private and not about you.
  22. the more the merrier: It's good to have more people join us.
  23. pick up on: To notice something small or hidden, like a feeling or a change.
  24. reach out to: To contact someone, often to offer or ask for help or just to say hello.
  25. rub someone the wrong way: If something rubs you the wrong way, it annoys you or feels not right, often without a clear reason.
  26. run in the family: A feature or habit is common in a family because it passes from parent to child.
  27. run into someone: To run into someone is to meet them by chance.
  28. see someone off: To go with someone to the place they are leaving from, so you can say goodbye.
  29. spill the beans: To tell a secret by accident.
  30. stand someone up: To not come to a meeting you agreed to, without telling the other person.
  31. tag along: To go somewhere with other people who didn't ask you.
  32. take a rain check: To say no to an invitation now and suggest doing it another time.
  33. take for granted: To take something for granted means to forget how lucky you are to have it, or to expect it will always be there.
  34. teacher's pet: A student the teacher likes best and treats better than the others.
  35. walk on eggshells: To be very careful with what you say or do so you don't upset someone.
  36. water under the bridge: Something unpleasant that happened in the past and is no longer important.
  37. write off: To decide that someone or something is not worth your time or has no chance.

How to use it: read the conversation out loud first, then do the four tasks. For the last task, answer out loud before you write, and try to use the phrase naturally.

bail on

Phrasal verbLevel B2Casual

To cancel a plan at the last minute, especially when others are counting on you.

Tip: Use 'bail on' + a person, event or plan (bail on dinner, bail on me); it's casual and can sound a little critical, so avoid it in formal writing.

KofiAre you coming to Olena's housewarming tonight?
AmiraI really want to, but I've had a awful week.
KofiShe's cooked for twelve people, though.
AmiraI know. I feel terrible, but I think I'm going to bail on her.
KofiThen text her now, not at seven o'clock.
AmiraGood point. I'll do it right away.

bang on about

Phrasal verbLevel B2Casual

To talk about the same subject for too long, in a boring or annoying way.

Tip: Use it with 'about' plus the subject (bang on about politics, bang on about work); it's mildly critical, so say it about other people more than to their face. It's also common in the form 'go banging on about'.

AmiraHow was the train ride with Kofi? Six hours is a long time.
KenHonestly, I lost the will to live around hour two.
AmiraWhy, what happened?
KenHe wouldn't stop banging on about his new running app. Steps, routes, heart rate, everything.
AmiraHa, I'd have put my headphones on straight away.
KenI did, eventually. He didn't even notice.

KofiGood idea. I'll get her one this afternoon.

OlenaGood idea. I'll chip in twenty, and I'll ask the others on the street.
KenGreat. I'll make a list so we know who's given what.
OlenaPerfect. Let's fix it before the next storm.

count me in

ExpressionLevel B1Casual

A friendly way to say you want to join an activity.

Tip: Say 'Count me in' when an invitation is open and you want to join; it's a short reply, so you don't add a verb after it. To say no politely, use 'Count me out'.

AmiraOlena and I are planning a picnic by the river on Sunday.
KofiNice idea! Is it just the two of you?
AmiraNo, anyone can come. We're meeting at noon, and everyone brings something to share.
KofiCount me in. I'll make my pasta salad.
OlenaPerfect! We'll bring the blankets and some games.
