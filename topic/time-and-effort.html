Time & effort

A conversational idiom workbook: 25 phrases, each with a short conversation and four quick tasks. Do it online and check your answers, or print it and use the answer key at the end.

In this workbook

  1. bite off more than you can chew: To try to do more than you can manage.
  2. chew through: To use up or finish a lot of something very quickly.
  3. come rain or shine: No matter what the weather is like, or whatever happens.
  4. easy peasy, lemon squeezy: Very, very easy. It is a playful, rhyming phrase.
  5. faff about: To waste time doing small, unimportant things instead of getting on with a task.
  6. fall by the wayside: To stop being done or looked after because other things became more important.
  7. get a workout in: To manage to fit exercise (or another activity) into a busy day.
  8. get around to: To finally do something you meant to do for a long time.
  9. get caught up in: To get caught up in something is to become so busy with it that you forget other things.
  10. give it a go: To try something, even if you aren't sure you will be good at it.
  11. give or take: 'Give or take' means about, with a small amount more or less.
  12. go to town: To do something with great energy and enthusiasm, without holding back.
  13. go, go, go: Very busy, with no time to rest.
  14. in the nick of time: Just before it was too late.
  15. lose sight of: To forget or stop thinking about what is most important.
  16. not just yet: Not now, but maybe soon.
  17. on the back burner: Something is not urgent, so you will deal with it later.
  18. a piece of cake: Something very easy to do.
  19. put a cap on it: To set a maximum limit on how much you do or use something.
  20. a rabbit hole: A topic or activity that pulls you in so deeply that you lose lots of time.
  21. run out of time: To have no time left; to use all your time.
  22. running late: To be behind schedule, so you will arrive later than planned.
  23. sleep on it: To wait until the next day to decide something.
  24. time to kill: If you have time to kill, you have free time while you wait for something.
  25. up in the air: Not decided yet; still uncertain.

How to use it: read the conversation out loud first, then do the four tasks. For the last task, answer out loud before you write, and try to use the phrase naturally.

bite off more than you can chew

IdiomLevel B2Casual

To try to do more than you can manage.

Tip: Use 'bite off more than you can chew' (often in the past: 'I think I bit off more than I could chew') when you took on too much work or responsibility; it's about commitments, not food.

OlenaYou look exhausted, Kofi. Are you okay?
KofiHonestly, no. I'm studying for my exam, working extra shifts, and I agreed to help my cousin move this weekend.
OlenaThat's a lot for one week.
KofiI know. I think I bit off more than I could chew this time.
OlenaMaybe ask your cousin if the move can wait until next week?
KofiGood idea. I'll text him tonight.

ExpressionLevel B1Neutral

AmiraOkay, then I'll bring extra socks and meet you there.

OlenaGood. Message me when it's done and I'll double-check it.
