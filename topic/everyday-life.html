Everyday life

A conversational idiom workbook: 13 phrases, each with a short conversation and four quick tasks. Do it online and check your answers, or print it and use the answer key at the end.

In this workbook

  1. by all means: A polite way to say 'yes, of course, please do'.
  2. clear up (weather): When the weather clears up, clouds, rain or fog go away and it gets brighter.
  3. come across: To find or meet something by chance.
  4. cut down on: To use or do less of something, usually to be healthier or save money.
  5. gathering dust: If something is gathering dust, nobody uses it any more.
  6. get rid of: To throw away or remove something you do not want or need.
  7. keep (someone) posted: To give someone new information regularly as a situation changes.
  8. on the receiving end of: The person who gets something, often something unpleasant.
  9. out of sight, out of mind: If you don't see something, you stop thinking about it.
  10. put (someone) off: To make someone not want to do something or not like something.
  11. to say nothing of: Used to add one more thing that is even more extreme than the thing you just mentioned.
  12. take advantage of: To make good use of a chance or situation. With a person, it means to treat them unfairly.
  13. up for grabs: Available for anyone to take or win.

How to use it: read the conversation out loud first, then do the four tasks. For the last task, answer out loud before you write, and try to use the phrase naturally.

by all means

ExpressionLevel B2Neutral

A polite way to say 'yes, of course, please do'.

Tip: Use it to give someone permission or to welcome a request, often followed by a command ("By all means, sit down"); don't use it to agree with an opinion.

OlenaDo you mind if I open the window? It's a bit stuffy in here.
KofiBy all means. I was thinking the same thing.
OlenaThanks. Oh, and is anyone sitting in this chair?
KofiNo, it's free. My sister is joining us later, but she can sit next to me.
OlenaGreat. I'll put my bag here, then.

clear up (weather)

Phrasal verbLevel B1Casual

When the weather clears up, clouds, rain or fog go away and it gets brighter.

Tip: Use it with 'it' or a weather word as the subject (the sky, the fog, the rain); it describes weather getting better, and you can add 'by' + a time to say when.

KofiIs the ferry still running? I can barely see the water this morning.
AmiraYes, but the fog is thick. They said it might not leave until ten.
KofiThat's a shame. I wanted to get to the island before the crowds.
AmiraLet's grab a coffee first. It usually starts to clear up around nine here.
KofiGood idea. If it does, we can still catch the second boat.

AmiraReally? I thought you'd lost it years ago.

cut down on

Phrasal verbLevel B1Neutral

To use or do less of something, usually to be healthier or save money.

Tip: Use it with 'on' plus the thing you reduce (cut down on coffee, cut down on spending); it's a gradual reduction, not stopping completely.

AmiraAre you getting another coffee? That's your third today.
KenI know, I know. I can't sleep properly lately.
AmiraMaybe you should cut down on caffeine in the afternoon.
KenYeah, you're right. Maybe I'll switch to water after lunch.
AmiraTry it for a week and see if you sleep better.
KenOkay, deal. This is my last one for today.

gathering dust

IdiomLevel B2Casual

If something is gathering dust, nobody uses it any more.

Tip: Use it with 'be' or 'sit' + 'gathering dust' (is/has been gathering dust) for things like gadgets, equipment or ideas that are no longer used; it's usually a little critical or regretful.

AmiraAre you still using that treadmill in the spare room?
DiegoHonestly, no. I bought it in January, and it's been gathering dust since March.
AmiraThat's a shame. My cousin is looking for one, actually.
DiegoReally? Tell them to come and get it. It's free if they can carry it.
AmiraI'll pass it on. It's better than leaving it there.

get rid of

keep (someone) posted

IdiomLevel B1Casual

To give someone new information regularly as a situation changes.

Tip: Use the pattern 'keep + person + posted' (keep me posted, keep them posted); it's often said as a friendly request when someone is waiting for news, and it's not used for one-off facts, so don't say 'keep me posted' when you only want a single answer.

OlenaMy flight's delayed again. They're saying another hour, maybe two.
KofiUgh, that's annoying. I was going to pick you up at six.
OlenaDon't leave yet. I'll check the board every so often.
KofiOkay, keep me posted and I'll head to the airport when you know more.
OlenaWill do. I'll text you as soon as they confirm a new time.

on the receiving end of

ExpressionLevel B2Neutral

The person who gets something, often something unpleasant.

Tip: Use it with 'be' and a noun or '-ing' phrase: 'on the receiving end of a complaint / of someone's anger'. It usually describes something negative, so avoid it for good news unless you are joking.

AmiraYou look tired. Was it a rough shift at the shop?
DiegoHonestly, yes. A customer was furious about a late delivery, and I was on the receiving end of it for twenty minutes.
AmiraOh no. Was it even your fault?
DiegoNot at all. The courier lost the parcel, but I was the only person standing there.
AmiraThat's so unfair. Let's get some food, and you can tell me everything.

out of sight, out of mind

IdiomLevel B2Casual

If you don't see something, you stop thinking about it.

Tip: Say it as a complete comment after something happens, often with a slightly humorous or resigned tone; it is a fixed phrase, so don't change the word order.

OlenaDid you finish the gym membership cancellation yet?
KofiNo, I keep forgetting. I only remember when I'm charged.
OlenaWhy don't you put the card in your bag instead of the kitchen drawer?
KofiBecause I never look in my bag. The card is in the drawer, but it's out of sight, out of mind.
OlenaThen set a reminder on your phone. Do it today!
KofiGood idea. I'll do it right now.

put (someone) off

Phrasal verbLevel B2Neutral

To make someone not want to do something or not like something.

Tip: Use 'put someone off' (or 'put someone off something/doing something') for things that kill your interest; the person goes in the middle: 'put me off', not 'put off me'. Don't confuse it with 'put off' meaning 'postpone'.

AmiraAre you still thinking about joining the gym on Main Street?
DiegoNot really. I went to look around yesterday, and the changing rooms were filthy.
AmiraOh no. That's disappointing, the prices are really good.
DiegoI know, but that completely put me off. I'd rather pay a bit more somewhere clean.
AmiraFair enough. There's a newer one near the station, if you want to check it out.

to say nothing of

ExpressionLevel B2Neutral

Used to add one more thing that is even more extreme than the thing you just mentioned.

Tip: Use it after a complaint or problem to add a bigger one, followed by a noun phrase or -ing form (to say nothing of the cost / of paying for parking); don't use it for good news that isn't extreme.

OlenaAre you going to take the car into the city for the concert on Saturday?
KofiI'd rather not. The traffic will be terrible, to say nothing of finding somewhere to park.
OlenaTrue. Last time I spent forty minutes just looking for a space.
KofiExactly. The train is slower, but at least it's cheap.
OlenaOkay, let's take the train. I'll check the times tonight.

take advantage of

ExpressionLevel B1Neutral

To make good use of a chance or situation. With a person, it means to treat them unfairly.

Tip: Use 'take advantage of' + a noun for a chance (take advantage of the offer); with a person, it sounds negative, so use it carefully.

KofiNo! We should take advantage of that and finally visit your sister.
OlenaGood idea. I'll book two seats before the offer ends.
KofiGreat. Can we leave early on Saturday? The mornings are quieter.
OlenaSure. I'll find a train that leaves around eight.

up for grabs

IdiomLevel B2Casual

Available for anyone to take or win.

Tip: Use it for things anyone can claim, like jobs, prizes, seats or leftover food; it's usually 'be up for grabs' or 'is/are up for grabs', and it sounds odd for people or formal contracts.

OlenaAre you taking the desk by the window? You've been sitting there all week.
KofiNo, it's not mine. Mei moved to the fourth floor yesterday.
OlenaSo nobody has claimed it yet?
KofiNot that I know of. I think it's up for grabs until the manager decides.
OlenaGreat, I'll move my laptop over now before someone else does.
KofiGood idea. That spot gets the best light.
