Everyday life

A conversational idiom workbook: 53 phrases, each with a short conversation and four quick tasks. Do it online and check your answers, or print it and use the answer key at the end.

In this workbook

  1. bits and bobs: Small, random things of different kinds.
  2. break the bank: To cost too much money or be more expensive than you can afford.
  3. bring up: To start talking about a topic.
  4. burn off: To use up calories or extra energy by doing exercise.
  5. burning a hole in your pocket: You have money and want to spend it right away.
  6. by all means: A polite way to say 'yes, of course, please do'.
  7. change it up: To do something differently so it does not get boring.
  8. chock full of: Completely full, with a lot of things inside.
  9. clear up (weather): When the weather clears up, clouds, rain or fog go away and it gets brighter.
  10. come across: To find or meet something by chance.
  11. cost an arm and a leg: To be very expensive.
  12. cut down on: To use or do less of something, usually to be healthier or save money.
  13. dial back: To reduce something, like how much you do or spend.
  14. dirt cheap: Extremely cheap, much less than you would expect.
  15. end up + -ing: To do something in the end, often without planning it.
  16. fit as a fiddle: Very healthy and strong.
  17. gathering dust: If something is gathering dust, nobody uses it any more.
  18. get by: To manage with just enough skill, money or knowledge.
  19. get rid of: To throw away or remove something you do not want or need.
  20. give someone a shout: To contact someone, usually by phone or message, to tell them something.
  21. go viral: If something goes viral, it spreads very fast online and millions of people see it.
  22. grab a bite: To have a quick, simple meal or snack.
  23. hang on to: To keep something safe for someone, or to not give it away.
  24. hang up: To hang up is to end a phone call.
  25. have a sweet tooth: To love sweet things like cake, candy and chocolate.
  26. hit or miss: Sometimes good and sometimes bad, so you can't predict the result.
  27. hit the road: To start a trip or leave a place.
  28. in the market for: Interested in buying a particular thing.
  29. keep an eye on: To watch something carefully to see if it changes.
  30. keep (someone) posted: To give someone new information regularly as a situation changes.
  31. off the top of my head: Said when you answer from memory, without checking or thinking for long.
  32. on the house: Given free by a restaurant or shop.
  33. on the receiving end of: The person who gets something, often something unpleasant.
  34. out of sight, out of mind: If you don't see something, you stop thinking about it.
  35. pass out: To fall asleep suddenly because you are very tired, or to faint.
  36. pay off: To finish paying all the money you owe for something.
  37. a pretty penny: A large amount of money.
  38. put (someone) off: To make someone not want to do something or not like something.
  39. raining cats and dogs: It is raining very hard.
  40. to say nothing of: Used to add one more thing that is even more extreme than the thing you just mentioned.
  41. shop around: To compare prices and choices in different places before you buy something.
  42. show up: To arrive, or to appear, especially when people are waiting for you.
  43. slip my mind: If something slips your mind, you forget it without meaning to.
  44. speaking of: A way to start a new comment that connects to the thing someone just mentioned.
  45. split the bill: To divide the cost between two or more people.
  46. stay away from: To avoid something or not go near it.
  47. stock up on: To buy a large amount of something so you have enough for later.
  48. take advantage of: To make good use of a chance or situation. With a person, it means to treat them unfairly.
  49. take it easy: To rest and not do too much.
  50. travel light: To travel with only a small amount of luggage.
  51. under the weather: Not feeling very well.
  52. up for grabs: Available for anyone to take or win.
  53. wait out: To stay where you are until something bad or unpleasant is over.

How to use it: read the conversation out loud first, then do the four tasks. For the last task, answer out loud before you write, and try to use the phrase naturally.

bits and bobs

IdiomLevel B2Casual

Small, random things of different kinds.

Tip: Say 'bits and bobs' as a plural noun phrase, often after 'some' or 'a few', or to finish a list of small items; it's more common in British English but widely understood.

KofiAre you all packed for the move?
AmiraNearly. The furniture is easy, but I still have a few bits and bobs to deal with.
KofiLike what?
AmiraPhone chargers, spare buttons, a tape measure. Nothing big, but I can't find a box for it all.
KofiI've got an empty shoebox you can use.
AmiraPerfect, that's exactly the size I need.
