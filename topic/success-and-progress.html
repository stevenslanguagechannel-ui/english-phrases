game changer

IdiomLevel B2Casual

Something that completely changes a situation, usually for the better.

Tip: Say 'a game changer' or 'a real game changer' for something that makes a big difference; it's often followed by 'for' + the person or situation affected, and it's usually positive.

AmiraHow's the new job going? Are you still driving for an hour each way?
DiegoNo, that's the best part. They let us work from home three days a week now.
AmiraThree days? That's a big change.
DiegoHonestly, it's a game changer. I have time to cook and go running again.
AmiraI'm jealous. My office still wants us there every day.

hit a plateau

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

When you hit a plateau, you stop making progress for a while.

Tip: Use it as 'hit a plateau' (or 'reach a plateau') about skills, results, or numbers; it is often followed by 'so' plus a change of plan, and the past tense is most common.

OlenaHow's the Spanish going? You were doing so well a few months ago.
DiegoHonestly, I think I've hit a plateau. I know the same words every week.
OlenaAre you still doing the same app lessons every day?
DiegoYes, the same ones. I just repeat them.
OlenaMaybe try talking to real people. That might get you moving again.
DiegoGood idea. There's a language café near my office on Thursdays.

make a go of it

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

To try hard to make something you have started become successful.

Tip: Use it as 'make a go of it' (or 'make a go of + noun') after you have already started something, like a business, a move or a relationship; it's often used with 'decide to' or 'want to'.

OlenaI heard you've been selling your candles at the weekend market.
AmiraYes, it started as a hobby, but people keep coming back for more.
OlenaSo are you going to keep it small?
AmiraNo, I've decided to make a go of it. I'm cutting my hours at the office to give it a proper chance.
OlenaThat's brave. Let me know if you need help with the website.

turn the tables

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

To reverse a situation so the other side is now in the weaker or stronger position.

Tip: Use it as a verb phrase: 'turn the tables on someone' or 'turn the tables' on its own, usually in the past tense when talking about a comeback or a role reversal.

KofiDid you hear about the quarter-final? Our team was down by twelve points at half-time.
OlenaTwelve? That sounds almost impossible to recover from.
KofiRight? But they changed their defence completely and turned the tables in the third quarter.
OlenaSo did they actually win?
KofiBy four points. The other team couldn't believe it.
