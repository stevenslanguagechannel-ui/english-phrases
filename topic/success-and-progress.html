A conversational idiom workbook: 11 phrases, each with a short conversation and four quick tasks. Do it online and check your answers, or print it and use the answer key at the end.

  2. get the hang of: To slowly learn how to do something.
  3. hit a plateau: When you hit a plateau, you stop making progress for a while.
  4. hold (someone) back: To stop someone from moving forward, or delay them.
  5. make a go of it: To try hard to make something you have started become successful.
  6. make bank: To earn a lot of money quickly, usually in an informal way.
  7. pass with flying colors: To pass a test very easily and with a very high score.
  8. pull off: To succeed at something difficult.
  9. scratch the surface: To learn or do only a very small part of something that is much bigger.
  10. take stock of: To stop and think carefully about what you have or where you are now.
  11. turn the tables: To reverse a situation so the other side is now in the weaker or stronger position.

How to use it: read the conversation out loud first, then do the four tasks. For the last task, answer out loud before you write, and try to use the phrase naturally.

game changer

IdiomLevel B2Casual

Something that completely changes a situation, usually for the better.

Tip: Say 'a game changer' or 'a real game changer' for something that makes a big difference; it's often followed by 'for' + the person or situation affected, and it's usually positive.

AmiraHow's the new job going? Are you still driving for an hour each way?
DiegoNo, that's the best part. They let us work from home three days a week now.
AmiraThree days? That's a big change.
DiegoHonestly, it's a game changer. I have time to cook and go running again.
AmiraI'm jealous. My office still wants us there every day.

get the hang of

IdiomLevel B1Neutral

To slowly learn how to do something.

Tip: Use it as 'get the hang of + noun' or 'get the hang of + -ing'; it often follows a first stage of struggling, and it's used for skills and routines, not facts.

OlenaHow's the new job going, Kofi?
KofiTiring! The scheduling software confused me all week.
OlenaOh no. Is it any better now?
KofiA bit. I think I'm starting to get the hang of it, though. Today I only needed help twice.
OlenaThat's progress. Give it another week and it'll feel easy.

hit a plateau

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

When you hit a plateau, you stop making progress for a while.

Tip: Use it as 'hit a plateau' (or 'reach a plateau') about skills, results, or numbers; it is often followed by 'so' plus a change of plan, and the past tense is most common.

OlenaHow's the Spanish going? You were doing so well a few months ago.
DiegoHonestly, I think I've hit a plateau. I know the same words every week.
OlenaAre you still doing the same app lessons every day?
DiegoYes, the same ones. I just repeat them.
OlenaMaybe try talking to real people. That might get you moving again.
DiegoGood idea. There's a language café near my office on Thursdays.

hold (someone) back

Phrasal verbLevel B1Neutral

To stop someone from moving forward, or delay them.

Tip: Use it as hold + person + back ("held me back"), usually when a problem, fear or person slows someone's progress, and don't mix it up with the literal "hold someone's arm".

MeiDid you sign up for the advanced Spanish class in the end?
KofiNot yet. I keep telling myself my speaking isn't good enough.
MeiBut you talk to your neighbour in Spanish every week.
KofiTrue, but I get nervous in front of strangers. That's what has held me back for months.
MeiThen sign up now. You'll know half the people after one lesson anyway.

make a go of it

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

To try hard to make something you have started become successful.

Tip: Use it as 'make a go of it' (or 'make a go of + noun') after you have already started something, like a business, a move or a relationship; it's often used with 'decide to' or 'want to'.

OlenaI heard you've been selling your candles at the weekend market.
AmiraYes, it started as a hobby, but people keep coming back for more.
OlenaSo are you going to keep it small?
AmiraNo, I've decided to make a go of it. I'm cutting my hours at the office to give it a proper chance.
OlenaThat's brave. Let me know if you need help with the website.

make bank

IdiomLevel B2Casual

To earn a lot of money quickly, usually in an informal way.

Tip: Use it as an informal verb phrase (make bank, is making bank, made bank) for people or businesses; it's slang, so avoid it in formal writing or serious reports.

KofiDid you hear Amira sold her old camera gear online?
MeiYes! She told me she made bank. Someone paid triple what she expected.
KofiWow. I have a drawer full of old phones and cables.
MeiCheck what they're worth before you throw them out. Some of that stuff sells fast.
KofiOkay, I'll look this weekend. Maybe I'll finally afford a new laptop.

pass with flying colors

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

To pass a test very easily and with a very high score.

Tip: Use it with 'pass' (or 'come through', 'get through') plus an exam, inspection or assessment: 'She passed the inspection with flying colors.' Don't use it for things you can't pass, like a holiday or a meal. The British spelling 'colours' is also correct.

OlenaKofi, you look relaxed. Did the food safety inspection go okay yesterday?
KofiBetter than okay. The inspector checked the whole kitchen and we passed with flying colors.
OlenaReally? Last year they found a few problems with the fridges.
KofiI know! This time we fixed everything beforehand, so there was nothing to write down.
OlenaThat's great news. The whole team deserves a treat.

pull off

Phrasal verbLevel B2Neutral

To succeed at something difficult.

Tip: Use it with an object: 'pull it off' (the pronoun goes in the middle), or 'pull off a big project'; it often follows 'manage to' or 'hope to' and suggests the task was risky or hard.

KenDid your team finish the product demo for the client?
AmiraYes! The software crashed an hour before, and we had to rebuild the slides from scratch.
KenWow, that sounds stressful. Did the client notice anything?
AmiraNot at all. Honestly, I'm amazed we pulled it off.
KenYou must be exhausted. Let's get lunch, my treat.

scratch the surface

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

To learn or do only a very small part of something that is much bigger.

Tip: Usually used as 'only scratching the surface (of something)' or 'barely scratched the surface'; it describes how much is still left to learn, see or do, so it fits big subjects, projects and places.

OlenaHow's the new data course going? You've been at it for a month now.
KofiI've finished six modules, but honestly I've only scratched the surface. There's a whole advanced track after this.
OlenaWow, that's a lot. Does it feel too big?
KofiA bit, but I like it. Every week I find a new tool I didn't know existed.
OlenaWell, you've already helped me clean up my spreadsheet, so something is working.

take stock of

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

To stop and think carefully about what you have or where you are now.

Tip: Use it with an object: 'take stock of the situation / what we have / where things stand'. It sounds natural in work talk, but it is also fine with friends when life is changing.

AmiraYou look tired, Kofi. Has the move been that bad?
KofiIt's chaos. Boxes everywhere, and I can't find anything.
AmiraBefore you unpack another thing, why not take stock of what you've actually got?
KofiGood idea. I probably own three kettles and no plates.
AmiraHa! Make a list, then keep only what you need.
KofiOkay. I'll do it this evening, room by room.

turn the tables

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

To reverse a situation so the other side is now in the weaker or stronger position.

Tip: Use it as a verb phrase: 'turn the tables on someone' or 'turn the tables' on its own, usually in the past tense when talking about a comeback or a role reversal.

KofiDid you hear about the quarter-final? Our team was down by twelve points at half-time.
OlenaTwelve? That sounds almost impossible to recover from.
KofiRight? But they changed their defence completely and turned the tables in the third quarter.
OlenaSo did they actually win?
KofiBy four points. The other team couldn't believe it.
