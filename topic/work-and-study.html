Work & study

A conversational idiom workbook: 29 phrases, each with a short conversation and four quick tasks. Do it online and check your answers, or print it and use the answer key at the end.

In this workbook

  1. breathing down your neck: Watching someone very closely, so they feel pressure.
  2. call it a day: To stop working for the day.
  3. catch up on: To do something you didn't have time to do earlier.
  4. double-check: To check something a second time to be sure it is right.
  5. drop the ball: To make a mistake by forgetting or failing to do something you should do.
  6. fill someone in: To give someone the latest information they missed.
  7. get off (work / a shift): To finish work for the day.
  8. get through: To finish something long or difficult.
  9. get to the meat of: To reach the most important part of something without extra detail.
  10. hit the books: To start studying seriously.
  11. the ins and outs: The ins and outs are all the small details of how something works.
  12. kick off: To start something, like a meeting, a project or an event.
  13. leeway: Extra freedom or flexibility that lets you do something a little differently or later.
  14. a leg up: An advantage that helps you do better than others.
  15. make up (work or time): To do work or time later because you missed it earlier.
  16. on my behalf: Instead of someone, or for them, as their representative.
  17. part and parcel: Something that is a normal, necessary part of a situation or job.
  18. pick someone's brain: To ask someone who knows a lot about a subject for advice or ideas.
  19. pull an all-nighter: To stay awake all night to study or finish work.
  20. side hustle: A small extra job you do in addition to your main job.
  21. slip up: To make a small mistake, often by not paying attention.
  22. slow on the uptake: Slow to understand something or to catch on.
  23. spread yourself thin: To try to do too many things at once, so you can't do any of them well.
  24. take credit for: To say, or let people believe, that you did something good.
  25. take the fall: To accept the blame for a mistake, especially one other people share.
  26. team up: To join someone and work together to do something.
  27. toe the line: To obey the rules and do what is expected.
  28. touch base: To contact someone briefly to share news or check how things are going.
  29. use it or lose it: If you don't use a skill or something you have, you will lose it.

How to use it: read the conversation out loud first, then do the four tasks. For the last task, answer out loud before you write, and try to use the phrase naturally.

breathing down your neck

IdiomLevel B2Casual

Watching someone very closely, so they feel pressure.

Tip: Use it with a person or group as the subject: 'someone is breathing down my neck' (usually while you are working on something), and don't use it for a literal breath.

MeiAre you still working on the report? It's nearly eight.
DiegoYeah, I'm stuck on the last section. The client keeps emailing me for updates.
MeiEvery hour?
DiegoPretty much. With them breathing down my neck, I can't think straight.
MeiTurn off your notifications for an hour. You'll finish faster.

call it a day

IdiomLevel B1Casual

To stop working for the day.

Tip: Use it as a fixed phrase: 'Let's call it a day' to suggest stopping, or 'We called it a day' for the past. It often follows a long effort, and it works at the end of any task, not only paid work.

OlenaAre you still working on the slides? It's nearly eight.
KofiYes, but my eyes are getting tired. The charts still look wrong.
OlenaYou've been staring at them since lunch. Why don't you call it a day?
KofiYou're right. I'll fix them first thing tomorrow.
OlenaGood idea. They'll make more sense after some sleep.

get to the meat of

IdiomLevel B2Neutral

Tip: Use it with 'the': 'get to the meat of' is followed by a noun like 'the problem', 'the report' or 'the issue', and people often say 'let's get to the meat of it' to move a conversation along.

OlenaSorry I'm late. Did I miss much of the budget call?
KofiOnly the introductions and some chat about the new software.
OlenaSo nothing important yet?
KofiRight. They're about to get to the meat of it now: who loses money and who gets more.
OlenaOkay, good. Let me turn my camera on and join in.
